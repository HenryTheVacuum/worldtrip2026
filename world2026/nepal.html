01 The time audit

Six days
is five and a half

Unusually for this trip, both flights are generous. Drukair's Paro service leaves at 07:10 and puts you on the ground at Tribhuvan around 08:10 Nepal time — a whole day, not a fragment. And Singapore Airlines' daily non-stop out of Kathmandu goes late, around 22:50, so the last day is a real one too. Five nights buys five full days and a long arrival day, which is more than the calendar suggests and still not much for a country this size.

No jet lag to speak ofBhutan runs UTC+6, Nepal UTC+5:45 — a fifteen-minute clock change and a seventy-five-minute flight. You arrive at your own body's normal hour for the first time since Toronto, which is worth remembering when you plan the alarm for Poon Hill.

Already part-acclimatisedParo sits at 2,235 m and you walked to Tiger's Nest at 3,120 m. Ghorepani is 2,860 m and Poon Hill is 3,210 m. Nothing on this leg is higher than what Bhutan already put you through, and that is the single best reason this stop sits exactly here in the order.

Sit on the right out of ParoFlying west, the Himalaya are on your northern side. On a clear November morning the PBH–KTM leg runs past Kanchenjunga, Makalu, Everest and Lhotse in sequence. It is the free version of the mountain flight everyone pays two hundred dollars for out of Kathmandu.

02 Three things to know first

One of these
is a real problem

The road between Kathmandu and Pokhara is currently severed, and everything about how you move on this leg follows from that. The other two are smaller but shape the first two days.

The road to Pokhara is cut

Live as of September 2026

On 30 August 2026 the flooded Trishuli took out roughly 70 metres of the Prithvi Highway at Krishnabhir in Dhading, with another 120 metres badly damaged. That highway is the artery between Kathmandu and Pokhara. Tourist buses have sat idle since; operators have been improvising a bypass over a suspension bridge at Jamire with a jeep shuttle to Benighat, and a temporary diversion track was under construction in early September.

The government said the section would be operational "within this month". No engineering timeline has been published. The realistic read is that it may well be fixed by mid-November — but it may be a fragile single-lane diversion rather than a highway.

What it means for youFly, and book early. Kathmandu–Pokhara passenger numbers went from about 1,300 a day on 25 August to over 3,500 within a week, and seats have been hard to get on Buddha, Yeti and Shree. Reserve both domestic legs the day you fix your dates, and recheck the highway a fortnight out.

You land on Bhai Tika

11 November, the last day of Tihar

Tihar 2026 runs 7–11 November, and Bhai Tika falls on your arrival day. It's a private family festival — sisters marking brothers with a seven-colour tika — so shops shut, offices close, and the streets go quiet in a way Kathmandu almost never does. The city is still garlanded and lit from Laxmi Puja three nights earlier.

The flip side: it's the tail of the busiest domestic travel week of the year, with the whole country moving home and back.

The leverDon't try to connect straight through to Pokhara on the 11th. Take the Kathmandu night. The city on Bhai Tika is genuinely worth having, and a morning domestic flight on the 12th is the safer bet anyway.

Mid-November is the best window Nepal has

Nothing to fix

The monsoon has washed the air, the harvest is in, and the post-monsoon sky is the most stable and clearest of the year — better even than October, and well before the winter inversion that puts Kathmandu in the world's ten most polluted cities by February. Expect 21–23°C by day in Kathmandu, 24°C in Pokhara, and around 10°C by day, −2 to −4°C at night at Ghorepani.

The cost is company. This is peak trekking season: Ghorepani's teahouses fill, and the permit checkpoints are at their strictest of the year.

Expectation settingPoon Hill at 05:45 in mid-November will have somewhere between thirty and a hundred people on it. That is normal, and it does not spoil it. Walk to the far end of the platform and let the tour groups cluster by the tower.

03 The open question

How much
trek fits

Kathmandu and Pokhara are fixed; the variable is how far up you go between them. The shortest honest Ghorepani–Poon Hill trek is three days on foot plus a flight and two jeep transfers, and it consumes the entire middle of the window. The alternative is a single night on a ridge at 2,060 m that costs one day instead of four.

Recommended

Poon Hill proper

Kathmandu ×1→ Ulleri ×1→ Ghorepani ×1→ Pokhara ×1→ Kathmandu ×1

The trek goes in the middle, deliberately. Fly to Pokhara on the morning of the 12th, walk up over two days, take the dawn on the 14th, and be back in Kathmandu by the evening of the 15th with a full final day in the valley.

Why the middle and not the end: the trek is the only weather-and-daylight-dependent thing on this leg. If a domestic flight cancels for morning fog on the 12th, you still have the 15th and 16th to absorb it. Reverse the order — Kathmandu first, trek last — and one cancellation eats Poon Hill entirely with no room to recover.

The cost is Kathmandu: one full day plus two evenings, which means Bhaktapur or the valley's Durbar squares, not both at leisure.

If three days on foot is too much

The low road

Kathmandu ×2→ Pokhara ×1→ Aus. Camp ×1→ Kathmandu ×1

Two proper valley days up front — Pashupatinath, Patan, Bhaktapur without a clock — then Pokhara, then a single night on the ridge at Australian Camp, 2,060 m, reached by a two-hour climb from Phedi. Sunset and sunrise over Annapurna South, Hiunchuli and Machhapuchhre from the same doorstep.

Add Sarangkot at dawn — a forty-minute drive from Lakeside, no walking at all — and you have seen essentially the same range twice for one day of effort instead of four.

What you give up is the thing itself: the 3,300-step staircase to Ulleri, the forest, the sleeping at 2,860 m, and Dhaulagiri and Annapurna I stacked side by side at 05:50. Australian Camp is a lovely view. Poon Hill is a different order of thing.

The version that doesn't fit, and why

The classic Poon Hill circuit descends via Deurali, Tadapani and Ghandruk rather than back down the staircase — a far better walk, through Gurung villages with the mountains in front of you the whole way. It needs a fourth and usually a fifth day on foot. On a five-night window with two domestic flights in it, there is no version of that which leaves you a day in Kathmandu.

So the three-day trek above is out and back the same way: up the Ulleri stairs, down the Ulleri stairs. The descent is around six hours and is harder on the knees than the climb was on the lungs. Take poles, and take the porter.

What's cut either way

Chitwan. Two nights minimum for the jungle to be worth the drive, and it's south, off the line entirely. Tanzania is on this itinerary in January; let that be the wildlife stop.

Annapurna Base Camp and Everest Base Camp. Seven and twelve days respectively, before contingency. Not a near miss — a different trip.

Langtang and Gosaikunda. Normal access through Syabrubesi has been disrupted by the same August floods; the Helambu alternative adds time you don't have.

Lumbini. The Buddha's birthplace, and a long way west of everything else here.

06 The climb

Two days up,
one dawn

Roughly 35 kilometres out and back from Birethanti, and about 2,200 metres of cumulative ascent. It is not technical and it is not high enough to be dangerous. It is, however, relentlessly vertical — the two kilometres between Tikhedhunga and Ulleri are a stone staircase of something like 3,300 steps, and that single stretch is what people remember.

What you actually see at 05:50Dhaulagiri (8,167 m) squarely west, Annapurna I (8,091 m) and Annapurna South to the north-east, Nilgiri between them, and Machhapuchhre's fishtail off to the east. The light hits Dhaulagiri first. There is a ₨100 fee at the top, collected in the dark.

AltitudeGhorepani at 2,860 m is below the altitude where acute mountain sickness usually starts, and you'll have come from Paro and Tiger's Nest. Day two is deliberately short for this reason — 900 m of gain and an early arrival. Drink more than you want to and don't skip the second night's sleep for a sunset.

ColdBelow freezing at Ghorepani after dark and colder on the ridge at dawn, with wind. Teahouse rooms are unheated plywood; the dining room has a stove and everyone sits in it. Down jacket, hat, gloves, and a head torch you have actually tested.

The forest, honestlyThis trail is famous for rhododendrons and you will see none. They flower in March and April; in November the trees are bare-branched and mossy and the walk is about the terraces, the waterfalls and the light instead. It's a fair trade for the clearest skies of the year.

07 The trek maths

Package or

A registered agency will run the three-day Poon Hill trek out of Pokhara for something like ₨45,000 per person (roughly CAD 450), covering guide, permits, transfers, rooms and food. Assembling the same thing yourself is cheaper — but not by as much as you'd expect, and the gap is the price of somebody else standing at the ACAP counter. Tick what you'd actually arrange.

The guide is not optional any more

Nepal Tourism Board rules require foreign trekkers in conservation areas and national parks to walk with a licensed guide booked through a government-registered agency. The rule dates from April 2023 and was tightened again in 2025; enforcement has always been patchy, but it is checked at Birethanti and Ghorepani and it is strictest in peak season, which mid-November is. Officials verify the guide's licence number and the agency's registration, and the sanction is being turned back.

On a leg with five nights and no slack, being sent back down from Birethanti costs you the entire stop. Book the agency. It is also the cheapest way to have the permits in hand before you reach Pokhara.

What a package quote usually doesn't include

The domestic flights. Kathmandu–Pokhara return is on you, and with the highway cut it's the expensive, scarce part of this leg. Budget well above the usual and book it first.

Your Pokhara hotel on the nights either side of the trek, and anything you eat in Lakeside.

Tips. ₨2,000–3,000 to the guide over three days is normal, more if you took a porter. Carry it in small notes; there is no ATM above Birethanti.

Gear. Down jacket, sleeping bag and poles all rent in Lakeside for a couple of dollars a day. Do not carry a four-season sleeping bag through twenty-six other countries for two nights in Ghorepani.

WeatherKathmandu 21–23°C by day and around 8°C at night; Pokhara 24°C and 11°C; Ghorepani roughly 10°C by day and −2 to −4°C after dark. Rain is barely a factor — the valley sees about 10 mm across the whole of November.

AirNovember is the valley's clean month. The bad season is late December through March, when Kathmandu regularly lands among the world's ten most polluted cities. Even so, mornings are always clearer than afternoons — another argument for early starts.

PashupatinathNon-Hindus cannot enter the main temple; the view is from the east bank of the Bagmati anyway, above the cremation ghats. Go early, dress conservatively, and don't photograph a cremation. It is somebody's family.

Water and foodRefill from boiled water at teahouses rather than buying bottles up the hill — it's cheaper and the plastic has nowhere to go. Order dal bhat; it comes with unlimited refills and everything else on a teahouse menu is a worse version of something you'd eat lower down.
