01 The time audit

Five days
is three days

Cape Verde is 1,500 km off the coast of Senegal and, from Cape Town, effectively behind Europe. There is no nonstop and no one-stop either — every routing runs north to Lisbon and back south-west, three flights and about thirty-two hours. Leave Cape Town on the evening of the 4th and you are on the ground in Praia in the small hours of the 6th. Two of four nights go to the journey before you have seen anything.

No jet lag, for onceCape Town is UTC+2 and Praia is UTC−1 — three hours back, the smallest clock change on the whole westbound run. The damage here is sleep debt from two red-eyes, not time-zone shift. The 6th is a slow day whatever you put on it.

One island, and that is the right callThe stop is Santiago and Tarrafal, both on the same island. On three days, adding Fogo or São Vicente would mean two domestic flights and a lost day each way. Santiago has the UNESCO site, the mountains and the best beach on the island — it is enough.

The airport is closeNelson Mandela International (RAI) sits about 15 minutes east of the Plateau. Taxi in, roughly 1,000–1,500 CVE. Nothing about this stop is lost to airport transfers, which is just as well given what the flights cost you.

02 Three things that don't move

Both ends
are broken

This is the only stop on the trip where the arrival and the departure are each a separate problem. Read all three before you book anything around it — the fix for the first one fixes the second for free.

There is no way to fly Cape Town → Praia

Breaks the stop · costs 2 of 4 nights

No nonstop, and — unusually for a 7,000 km hop — no one-stop either. Praia's only intercontinental gateways are Lisbon, Casablanca, Dakar and Bissau, and Cape Town flies to none of them. TAP's Johannesburg–Lisbon nonstop, the obvious African-side shortcut, was discontinued in March 2025.

Every workable routing is three flights: Cape Town overnight to a European hub, on to Lisbon, then TAP's 20:50 Lisbon–Praia. Roughly 32 hours door to door, landing about 00:20 on 6 February.

The fixSwap stops 25 and 26. Do Morocco first, then Cape Verde, then Lisbon. Casablanca–Praia is a nonstop 3h50 on Royal Air Maroc, and Praia–Lisbon is a nonstop 4h20 run one to two times a day — it also deletes the current plan's double backtrack. Both countries are dry and mild across the whole 4–14 Feb span, so nothing is lost seasonally. Full write-up filed at flag-capetown-praia-no-route.

Nothing leaves Praia in daylight

And nothing leaves on Monday the 8th

Every Royal Air Maroc departure for Casablanca goes in the middle of the night: AT594 at 01:20 (Fri, Sun) and AT593 at 03:50 (Tue, Thu), both nonstop at 3h35. There is a third, AT591 at 01:20 (Mon, Wed, Sat), but it is a one-stop and takes six hours.

The itinerary says fly out on Monday 8 February. The only Monday departure is that 01:20 one-stop — which means leaving for the airport on Sunday night and losing the whole of the 8th.

The fix, if you keep the orderTake AT593 at 03:50 on Tuesday 9 Feb instead. You keep all of Monday, go to the airport around midnight, and land in Casablanca about 09:25 — Morocco starts a few hours later than written, with one night's sleep spent in seat 14A. Filed at flag-praia-casablanca-night-departures.

Carnival is one day past the window

A near miss, not a disaster

Cape Verdean carnival is a serious national event, and 2027's peak — Shrove Tuesday, 9 February — falls the day after this window closes. Mindelo's parades run 7–14 Feb; Praia's own carnival builds over the same weekend at a smaller scale.

As written you catch the 7th and 8th: the build-up, the rehearsals, the street sound systems. You miss the day itself by about eighteen hours.

The leverIf carnival matters, it argues for the swap plus an earlier Cape Verde start — Morocco 4–7 Feb, Cape Verde 8–14 Feb, in Praia for Carnival Monday and Tuesday. That costs Morocco three of its six days, which is a real price. Filed at flag-capeverde-carnival-2027.

What to confirm before committing to any of them

Royal Air Maroc's actually-operated days. Schedule sites disagree on this route — one lists two Praia–Casablanca departures a week, another lists four across three flight numbers. The pattern above (AT593 Tue/Thu nonstop, AT594 Fri/Sun nonstop, AT591 Mon/Wed/Sat one-stop) is the best reconciliation of the sources, and it has no Monday nonstop in any of them. Pull real operated history for a few weeks around early February before you build a plan on a specific day.

The Cape Town exit. If you keep the written order, confirm that Cape Town → European hub → Lisbon can be bought on one ticket, and what the minimum connection is after an overnight. If you swap, the same question applies to Cape Town → Casablanca — Air France via Paris is the cleanest candidate, and CDG terminal transfers are slow.

06 The carnival question

Eighteen hours
short

Easter 2027 falls on 28 March, which puts Ash Wednesday on 10 February and Shrove Tuesday on 9 February. Cape Verde's carnival is not a resort event — Mindelo's is the big one, descended from Portuguese and Brazilian tradition, but every island runs its own, and the country slows down for the week around it.

What you'd actually see in Praia, as opposed to Mindelo

Mindelo, on São Vicente, is the one with the samba-school-scale parades, the costume workshops and the international coverage — and it is two domestic flights away from this itinerary. Praia's carnival is smaller and rougher: neighbourhood groups, sound trucks on the Plateau's avenues, a lot of drinking, and far fewer visitors.

For three days on Santiago the honest read is that carnival is a bonus if the dates fall your way and not a reason to restructure the trip — unless you want Mindelo specifically, and Mindelo is a different stop, not a different day.

The practical cost of being near it: rooms and inter-island flights across the whole archipelago tighten from late January, and prices on Santiago rise for carnival week even though the main event is elsewhere. If any shape puts you in Cape Verde between 6 and 11 February, book the room early.

07 Why February works

Bone dry,
and windy

This is one of the few stops on the trip where the calendar is straightforwardly right. Praia in February runs about 23°C by day and 21°C by night, with a 3–6% chance of a wet day and sea temperature around 23°C. The rain here is compressed almost entirely into August, September and October — you are at the opposite pole of the year.

Fig. 3 — Chance of a wet day in Praia, by month February marked

Indicative monthly averages — Cape Verde's entire rainfall arrives in a short late-summer window

The wind is the catchThe north-east trades blow steadily at around 26 km/h through February, from the north 80-odd percent of the time. It keeps the heat pleasant and it also means the north and east coasts get chop. Tarrafal sits in the lee on the north-west corner, which is exactly why its bay is the calm one.

Haze, not cloudLate winter brings the tail of the harmattan — Saharan dust carried out over the Atlantic. On a bad day the horizon goes milky and the mountains flatten out. It does not rain on you and it does not ruin anything, but it is the reason distant views here are less sharp than the temperature suggests.

The lightSunrise around 07:00, sunset around 18:35, eleven and a half hours of daylight. Short days by trip standards — the 6am starts that carried the Asia legs don't buy you anything here. Plan around the afternoon instead.

Water23°C, which is swimmable without being warm. Tarrafal's bay is the sheltered swim; Praia's Quebra Canela and Gamboa are fine but urban, and the Atlantic on the exposed coasts has real current.

08 What the ground costs

Two fees
you can't dodge

The escudo is pegged to the euro at 110.265 CVE = €1 and has been for decades, so euros are widely accepted and the arithmetic never moves. Two charges are compulsory: the airport security tax of 3,400 CVE per international arrival, paid through the EASE portal before you fly, and the tourist tax of 276 CVE per person per night, collected by your accommodation. Everything else below is a choice.

How money actually works here

Cards are patchy outside Praia. The capital's hotels, better restaurants and supermarkets take cards; aluguers, markets, park entries and most of Tarrafal do not. Draw escudos at an ATM in the Plateau on day one and carry a few thousand — Santiago is not an island where you want to be hunting a machine at six in the evening.

Euros work, change comes in escudos. Paying in euros is normal and the rate you get is the peg, so you lose nothing on the note — you just get change in a currency you can't spend after the 8th. Spend down before the airport.

The security tax is not collected at the airport. Pay it online with the EASE pre-registration. Arriving without either is the classic Cape Verde mistake and it is resolved slowly, in a queue, at the counter.
