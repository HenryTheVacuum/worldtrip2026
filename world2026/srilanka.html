01 The arithmetic

Four days,
four cities

Colombo, Kandy, Ella and Galle is a circuit of roughly 700 km on mountain roads, and the itinerary gives it four nights and three full days. Sri Lanka is a small island that travels slowly: Ella down to Galle alone is five and a half hours of switchbacks. Something has to come out, and the honest question is which thing.

Fig. 1 — Where the three usable days go 36 waking hours · moving vs. arrived

In a car, a bus or a trainActually somewhere

The window is really three daysYou land on the 20th and fly to Malé on the 24th. Arrival afternoon is a write-off and departure morning is a drive to the airport, so the usable block is 21, 22 and 23 December — call it 36 waking hours. Every hour in the fifth column below comes out of that number.

Kandy is the natural cutNot because it's the weakest — the Temple of the Tooth is the most important building in the country — but because it's the one that costs the most to include. Kandy sits three and a half hours from the airport and five and a half from Galle, and since the railway is broken it can no longer hand you off to the hill country by train.

Book a car and driver, not a carRoughly USD 60–80 a day including the driver, and it is the standard way visitors move here. Self-driving needs a temporary Sri Lankan permit and the roads — buses that overtake blind, tuk-tuks with right of way by conviction — are not a place to learn.

02 The thing you came for

The Kandy–Ella
train doesn't
run yet

This is the one material change on the leg, and it needs saying plainly. Cyclone Ditwah hit Sri Lanka at the end of November 2025 and wrecked the upcountry main line in 223 separate places. Nearly a year on the line is still being stitched back together, and as of September 2026 you cannot ride Kandy to Ella. What you can ride is the best part of it.

And this is the stretch every photograph you've seen of this train was taken on: out of Nanu Oya through Great Western and Idalgashinna, along the ridge at Haputale with the plains dropping away on both sides, then down the Demodara spiral into Ella. Two and a quarter to two and a half hours.

Two departures a day from Nanu Oya, around 09:00 and 14:30. Third class runs a few hundred rupees; second-class reserved about LKR 4,000; the first-class observation saloon about LKR 5,000 — roughly CAD $22 for what is routinely called one of the best train rides on earth.

Sit on the leftTravelling Nanu Oya→Ella, the left-hand side gets the valley. Reverse it coming back.

Kandy → Nanu Oya is not

Target 31 Dec 2026 — after you leave

The gap runs roughly Hatton to Nanu Oya, where the Kotagala–Talawakelle section and the ground near Great Western station were still under restoration through mid-2026. Railway authorities have set 31 December 2026 as the completion date for the full Kandy–Badulla line — eleven days after you land, on a deadline that has already slipped from April and then from June.

Separately, Colombo Fort to Kandy via Peradeniya was announced in early September for a October 2026 restart, with the Rambukkana–Kadugannawa reconstruction near done. That one looks likely; the upcountry stretch is the one to doubt.

What you do insteadDrive to the train. Kandy→Nanu Oya is 2½–3 hours by hired car (USD 35–50), Colombo→Nanu Oya about 4 hours. Then board at Nanu Oya. You lose the dull half of the ride and keep all of the good half.

Booking the seat

Set an alarm for 20 November

Reservations open exactly 30 days before travel, at 09:00 Sri Lanka time, on the railway's own portal at seatreservation.railway.gov.lk. First-class observation seats go within hours; second-class reserved lasts longer. For a 21 December train that means 21 November, and for a 22nd, the 22nd.

If you miss it, third class is unreserved, cheap, and available on the day — and standing in an open doorway with your feet over the drop is, honestly, the version most people remember. Just don't count on a seat.

Before you flyRecheck railway.gov.lk. This picture has moved every few weeks all year and could easily be better — or worse — by December.

03 Three ways to spend it

Pick two
of the four

All three fit 20–24 December. The difference is what you're optimising for: the railway, the country's spiritual centre, or arriving in the Maldives without needing a holiday from your holiday.

Recommended

The train

Negombo ×1→ Ella ×1→ Galle ×2→ CMB

Sleep by the airport, drive straight up to Nanu Oya the next morning, take the afternoon train into Ella, and give the following morning to Little Adam's Peak and the Nine Arch Bridge before the long descent to Galle. Two nights in the fort at the end.

Kandy is the price. You'll pass within twenty minutes of it on the drive up and not stop. In exchange you get the railway — the reason this stop is on the itinerary — plus a real, unhurried Galle rather than a four-hour visit to it.

Kandy & the coast

Straight up to Kandy on arrival, a full day for the Temple of the Sacred Tooth and Peradeniya's botanical gardens, then the long transfer south. Only two bases and two moves — the calmest shape on paper.

The counter-argument

South only

Colombo ×1→ Galle ×3→ CMB

An evening on Galle Face Green, a morning in Pettah, then the coastal train south — untouched by the cyclone, running normally, two hours with the sea close enough to spray the windows. Three nights in one place, whales off Mirissa at dawn, and nothing else asked of you.

This is not the lazy option, it's the argued one. Late December is the hill country's fifteenth-wettest-day-in-a-month season, the Maldives leg after this is five days of water, and you're eleven stops from home. There's a real case for treating Sri Lanka as the coast and coming back for the mountains.

Why the recommendation keeps the train

Because it's the specific thing this stop exists for, and because the part that survived the cyclone is the part worth having. The Kandy–Nanu Oya section you're missing runs through Nawalapitiya and Hatton — pretty, but it's the approach; the ridge from Nanu Oya to Ella is the performance. A three-hour car ride to reach a two-and-a-half-hour train is a bad-sounding trade that delivers the whole of what you wanted.

The case against is the 5½-hour Ella→Galle descent that follows it, which is the single ugliest leg on this itinerary. If that one drive is the thing that breaks you, take the third shape and don't feel bad about it — Sri Lanka is a country to return to, not to speed-run.

What's cut in every version

The Cultural Triangle. Sigiriya (USD 35), Dambulla, Polonnaruwa and Anuradhapura are four hours north of Colombo in the opposite direction from everything above. They need three days of their own. Nothing about a four-day window makes them possible.

Adam's Peak. The pilgrimage season opens on Unduvap Poya — which this year is 23 December, inside your window — but it's a 5,500-step overnight climb from a trailhead five hours from anywhere you'll be. Wrong trip.

Yala and Udawalawe. The leopard parks are on the way from Ella to the south coast, and a safari means a 05:00 start plus most of a day. You have three days total.

The east coast. Trincomalee and Arugam Bay are the wrong half of the island in December — the northeast monsoon puts the rain there and the surf is a mess. This is the one thing the calendar is unambiguously right about.

06 The weather claim, checked

December is
the turn, not
the peak

The itinerary says December opens the southwest's dry season, and that's true — but it's the first clean month after the worst two, not the best month of the year. Colombo goes from 395 mm across 19 days in October and 365 across 17 in November to 174 across 10 in December. That's the curve breaking. January and February, at 85 and 70 mm, are where it bottoms out, and you're three weeks early for that.

The coast gets the good version

Galle in late December is close to ideal

Around 31°C by day and 23°C at night on the southwest coast, sea at 28°C and calm, with rain arriving as short heavy bursts rather than the all-day grey of October. Ten rainy days across the month means a roughly one-in-three chance any given afternoon catches one.

The second half of December is better than the first, and the south coast is where the country's own tourist season is aimed. Whale season off Mirissa opens this month and runs to March.

The hills don't get the same deal

Plan for mist

Nuwara Eliya's December is 183 mm across 15 days — barely down from November's 226, because the northeast monsoon that's drying the southwest is still throwing weather at the eastern slopes of the highlands, and Ella sits on the wrong side of that line.

What this actually means: green, dramatic, waterfalls at full flow, and a real chance your train ride or your dawn hike is inside a cloud. It also means 11°C nights at Nuwara Eliya — the coldest air of the entire 27-country trip, and nothing in a Southeast Asia packing list covers it.

The leverGo early, again. Hill mornings are clearer than hill afternoons almost every day of the year, which is another argument for the 09:00 train over the 14:30 one if you can restructure the drive.

Christmas week is the price spike

Book by October

20 December to 5 January is Sri Lanka's peak: rooms in Galle Fort and along the south coast run at double or triple their November rate, and the good ones inside the walls go months out. Whale boats, drivers and the better restaurants all want reserving.

You are landing on the exact day the surge starts. Nothing to do about it except book early and expect the fort's lanes to be busy at sunset.

What to wear whereShoulders and knees covered at the Temple of the Tooth and any working temple, shoes off before you enter, and never pose with your back to a Buddha image — it's a genuine offence here, not a guidebook nicety. The coast is entirely relaxed by comparison.

Packing for 11°CNuwara Eliya at night is the coldest you'll be between Nepal in November and Morocco in February. One fleece and a shell, which you're carrying anyway, and unheated hill guesthouses will hand you a hot water bottle.

EatingRice and curry is not one dish — it's a plate of rice ringed by five or six small ones, with pol sambol and papadum. Kottu is the late-night thing, announced by the sound of two blades on a griddle from a street away. Both are usually under LKR 1,000.

Water and mosquitoesDon't drink the tap water; bottled and filtered are everywhere. Dengue is present year-round and worse after rain, which December has had plenty of — repellent in the evenings, especially in Colombo and on the coast.
