01 The time audit

Four days
is two

The itinerary reads 4–7 November. Nov 4th is an arrival evening off a Taipei flight; Nov 7th is a 06:00 departure to Paro, which means you're up at 03:00 and gone before Bangkok's markets have opened their shutters. What's left in the middle is two full days — Thursday and Friday — and everything below is built against that number, not the four on the calendar.

No jet-lag gift this timeTaipei and Bangkok sit one hour apart. Unlike the run into Japan, there's no free 5am energy here — if you're out late in Chinatown on Friday night, you feel it at the 03:00 alarm on Saturday. Discipline has to be yours; the clock won't hand it to you.

Same airport in and outThe one piece of good news: you land and depart from Suvarnabhumi both ends, no cross-city transfer like Hakone or the Kyoto shuffle. That buys back some of what the market and the museum lose.

The 06:00 is not negotiableDrukair and Bhutan Airlines fly BKK–Paro only in the morning — Paro sits at 2,300m ringed by mountains, and approach and departure both need daylight and calm air. Drukair's KB131 leaves at 06:00, lands 08:15. There's a second carrier flight around 06:30, but neither gives you a lie-in.

02 Two things that don't move

Whichever way
you slice it

One is a scheduling conflict with no clean fix, the other is a matter of honest expectations about the weather. Worth knowing before you build the day plan.

Chatuchak: closed when you're free

Doesn't fit this leg

Chatuchak Weekend Market runs its full 15,000-stall spread only on Saturday and Sunday, 9am–6pm — Wednesday and Thursday it's plants-only, Friday evening is wholesale. Your one Saturday here is 7 November, and by 9am you've been at Suvarnabhumi for six hours already, boarding for Paro. There is no version of this trip where you walk into Chatuchak.

The consolationOr Tor Kor Market, directly across the road from Chatuchak's gate, is open daily and is by most accounts the better market anyway — higher-quality produce, ready-to-eat stalls, none of the weekend crush. It's the honest swap, not a downgrade.

Early November: the tap is turning off, not off yet

A real improvement, not a guarantee

Bangkok's rain drops hard through autumn: September averages around 244mm, October 196mm, November about 46mm — already a fraction of the wet-season months. But it isn't the bone-dry stretch either; December falls to roughly 10mm and stays low into February.

Daytime highs barely move — low 30s°C from October straight through November — so "coolest" here is really about humidity and the odds of an afternoon downpour dropping, not a temperature change you'll feel.

The leverWhat rain remains tends to arrive as a short, heavy, late-afternoon burst rather than an all-day soak. Front-load temples and the canal tour into the morning and you're mostly clear of it.

Two changes since this trip was booked

Visa-free stay cut from 60 to 30 days, effective 15 September 2026 — one month before Japan, five weeks before you land here. Canada is on the 30-day list (not the 15-day list, which is just Seychelles and Mauritius). For a three-night stop this changes nothing practically, but it's the kind of headline that's easy to plan against out of date.

Thailand Digital Arrival Card (TDAC) has been mandatory since May 2025 for every arrival. It's free, online, and the window to submit it opens exactly 72 hours before you land — not before you leave Taipei. Do it from the gate lounge or the hotel the night before; doing it a week out simply won't be accepted yet. Keep the QR code screenshotted in case the wifi at immigration is slow.

03 The real decision

Where you sleep
on Friday night

With only three nights and a 03:00 wake on the last one, where you're staying that final night matters more than which neighbourhood you pick for the first two. Missing KB131 doesn't cost you a day — Drukair's Bangkok–Paro service runs a handful of times a week, and the entire Bhutan leg sits behind this one flight.

Recommended

Central, then the airport

Sukhumvit or Old Town ×2→ Airport hotel ×1→ KB131

Two nights properly in the city — Old Town for the temples, or Sukhumvit for food and rooftop bars — then move Friday evening to somewhere like the Novotel Bangkok Suvarnabhumi, which connects to the terminal by a covered walkway. Your 03:00 becomes a lift ride, not a Grab booking in Friday-night traffic.

The cost is real but small: pack up once, eat dinner near the airport instead of downtown, lights out by 21:00. Given what's riding on 06:00, that's a trade worth making.

WeatherExpect 30–31°C by day and 23–24°C at night, humidity still noticeably higher than the December–February peak season. Pack for sun and a possible short downpour, not for the cool evenings you'll get in Hong Kong or Hanoi in a few weeks.

SIM / eSIMAn eSIM bought before you land (AIS or TrueMove) or a physical SIM from a 7-Eleven counter at the airport both work fine — Suvarnabhumi's arrivals hall has several counters and it's a five-minute stop.

TippingNot expected but appreciated for good service; rounding up is normal, and upscale restaurants often add a 10% service charge already.

Exchange1 CAD ≈ 24 THB. Airport exchange rates are mediocre; SuperRich and Vasu Exchange counters in the city are consistently better if you're carrying cash to change.
