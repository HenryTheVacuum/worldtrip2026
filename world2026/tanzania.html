01 Three things that need fixing

Both ends
of this stop
are broken

Zanzibar is the easiest week on the whole itinerary once you are standing on it. Getting on and off the island as written does not work: the flight in doesn't fly on the day you arrive, and the flight out doesn't fly on the day you leave. Both are fixable without moving a single date. The third card is a correction to the reason this stop was scheduled here at all.

The exit flight is Saturdays only

Breaks the handoff to Cape Town

Airlink opened the first-ever nonstop Cape Town–Zanzibar on 3 October 2026 — 4Z356/4Z357 on an Embraer E195-E2, roughly six hours, no Johannesburg. It is a beautiful piece of timing for this trip and it is once a week, on a Saturday.

Your handoff to stop 24 is Thursday 28 January 2027. The nonstop does not operate. The next one is Saturday 30 January, which costs two of Cape Town's seven days to save four hours of transit — a bad trade on the stop that needs settled weather more than this one does.

The fix — same day, same airlineAirlink also flies ZNZ→JNB as 4Z11, Mondays and Thursdays, 12:00→14:55. Thursday 28 January is a scheduled day. Clear South African immigration at Johannesburg, re-check onto any of the hourly JNB→CPT departures, and you are in Cape Town by mid-evening on the correct date, with a Zanzibar morning still in hand. Roughly 9½ hours door to door against the nonstop's 6.

No Thursday flight out of Cairo either

Already flagged on the Egypt page

EgyptAir's nonstop CAI–ZNZ (MS819) runs Wednesday, Friday and Sunday — the third weekly frequency was added from 16 December 2026. Thursday 21 January, the day this stop is meant to start, has no departure.

The recommended fix there is the Wednesday-night MS819, dep Cairo 22:35, arr Zanzibar 05:15 on Thursday 21 January. Tanzania's arrival date is unchanged; Egypt loses only its final evening.

What it buys you hereA dawn arrival instead of an afternoon one. Landing at 05:15 with a one-hour time shift from Cairo means day one is a real day, not a write-off — which is why the instrument panel above says seven full days out of eight calendar days. That is the best day-yield of any stop on this trip.

January is the hot season, not the dry one

A correction, not a problem

The plan says "hot, dry and clear before the long rains arrive in March." Two of three. January averages 53 mm across about 10 rain days at 75% humidity, with highs near 30°C and lows near 26°C. Zanzibar's genuinely dry season is June–October, when the same station records 32–34 mm.

What January actually is: the short hot gap between the vuli short rains that end in December and the masika long rains that start in March, when April alone takes 204 mm. Rain here arrives as a violent half-hour and then leaves. It is not a season that cancels days.

What you actually getSea at 28°C — the warmest of the year — and the kaskazi north-east monsoon, which is what makes January the kitesurfing season on the east coast. Pack for heat and one soaking, not for weather.

Why Johannesburg and not Nairobi

Kenya Airways will sell you ZNZ→NBO→CPT on a Thursday in about 9h55, and Ethiopian will do it via Addis. Both hubs sit on South Africa's yellow-fever risk list, and South Africa applies the IHR rule that an airport transit of more than 12 hours in a risk country triggers the certificate requirement. A short connection is fine; a delayed one that pushes you past twelve hours in Nairobi is a problem at Cape Town immigration that you cannot solve from inside the terminal.

Johannesburg is South African soil, so the question never arises. It is also the densest domestic air corridor in Africa — Airlink, FlySafair, SAA and Lift all fly JNB–CPT many times a day, so a misconnection costs you an hour, not a night. Book the ZNZ–JNB and JNB–CPT legs on one ticket if Airlink will sell it that way, so the connection is their problem rather than yours.

Still to confirm at booking

1. Airlink 4Z11's operating days for late January 2027. Mon/Thu is the published year-round pattern, and a peak-season Tuesday/Wednesday extra ran 15 Dec 2026 – 13 Jan 2027 — which ends before your window, so you are back on the base schedule. Verify against actually-operated dates rather than a schedule-site day grid.

2. The Cape Town nonstop's timing. Airlink's own release gives 4Z357 as ZNZ 15:30 → CPT 20:50; one aggregator lists 15:15 → 19:45. Immaterial unless you switch to the Saturday, but worth knowing the sources disagree.

3. Minimum connection at JNB. You arrive international and depart domestic, which means immigration, baggage reclaim and re-check. Two hours is the floor; three is comfortable.

03 The open question

Seven days
of island,
or five

This is the only stop on a 27-country itinerary that lands inside a world-class wildlife event. Late January is the peak of the wildebeest calving season on the southern Serengeti's short-grass plains around Ndutu — roughly half a million calves born in a three-week window, with every predator on the ecosystem following them. You are 700 km and one light aircraft away from it, on the right dates, by accident.

Recommended

Zanzibar entire

Stone Town ×2→ North coast ×4→ Stone Town ×1→ ZNZ

Two nights inside the Stone Town labyrinth at the start, four on the north coast where the tide doesn't take the sea away, and a last night back in town so the 12:00 departure is a fifteen-minute taxi rather than an hour and a half.

The case for it: you are twenty-two stops into a world trip. Zanzibar is the first place on the route where doing nothing is the correct use of a day, and seven days is exactly enough to stop moving. It is also the cheap shape — the island runs on roughly $120–250 a day mid-range, against a safari's daily park fees alone.

If the calving is worth $2,000

Ndutu split

Stone Town ×2→ Ndutu ×2→ North coast ×2→ Stone Town ×1→ ZNZ

Coastal Aviation and the other bush carriers fly ZNZ direct to the Ndutu airstrip — around 2h40 with a hop en route, from roughly $350 each way. Two nights at a Ndutu camp puts you on the plains for two full dawns, which is the minimum that justifies the airfare.

The real cost isn't the flight. Serengeti conservation fees run $82.60 per adult per 24 hours, plus a $59 per night concession fee at any lodge or camp inside the park, before the camp's own rate. Two nights all-in, flights included, lands somewhere around $1,800–2,500 per person — more than this entire week costs otherwise.

Why the recommendation stays on the island, and the honest counter-argument

The case for Zanzibar-entire is arithmetic and fatigue. Two thousand dollars is a meaningful fraction of what several later stops cost in total, and the split turns a seven-day rest into a four-day one with two airport days inside it. Stop 24 is Cape Town, which is a walking, climbing, driving week — arriving at it already tired is a real cost.

The counter-argument is that this doesn't come round again. The calving is a fixed three-week annual event and you are inside it without having planned to be; the rest of the itinerary contains no other large-mammal window at all. If there is one place on Westbound to break the budget, the argument that it is this one is not a weak argument. The deciding question is whether the money is there — not whether it is worth it.

If you do go: the calving concentrates on the short-grass plains around Ndutu, which straddle the Serengeti boundary and the Ngorongoro Conservation Area, not on the central Seronera plains most itineraries default to. Book a camp that is actually at Ndutu, and confirm which side of the boundary it sits on, because the fee regime differs.

What's cut in both shapes

Kilimanjaro. Five to nine days on the mountain. Not a question this trip can ask.

Pemba. Zanzibar's quieter northern sister, better diving, almost no tourism. A 20-minute flight and a completely different island — but it needs three nights of its own to be worth the transfer, and those three nights come out of Unguja.

Dar es Salaam. Two hours by ferry, and there is little reason to spend a day of this window on it.

06 The coast question

North, because
of the tide

Zanzibar's single most consequential practical fact, and the one most people learn on arrival: on the east coast the sea leaves. Twice a day, for hours, the water withdraws a kilometre or more across the reef flat, and swimming stops being possible. The north-west coast sits outside that and stays deep all day.

Nungwi & Kendwa — the northDeep water at any hour, no tidal exposure worth planning around, sunsets that face west over open sea. This is where the dhow-at-sunset picture comes from and it is the correct base for a stop that has four coastal nights and no interest in rearranging them around a tide table. The trade is atmosphere: Nungwi is built up, busy and resort-heavy, with a strip of bars behind the beach.

Paje & Jambiani — the eastA different and in some ways better place: seaweed farmers working the flats at low water, kitesurfers filling the lagoon when the kaskazi blows, a long thin ribbon of guesthouses and reggae bars, and far more of a sense that people live here. January is peak kite season. But you swim when the tide says so, and if you are not kiting, half your days have a hole in them.

Matemwe — the compromiseNorth-east, opposite Mnemba, quieter than both, with the island's best snorkelling on the doorstep. Still tidal, though less brutally than Paje. The right answer if the water is the reason you came and you do not want Nungwi's noise.

Check the tide table before you book a boat, not after

Everything on the water here is tide-locked, not clock-locked. Mnemba snorkelling boats leave when there is water over the reef. Safari Blue's sandbank only exists at low tide. The Nakupenda sandbank west of Stone Town is the same. A dhow sunset cruise out of Nungwi is the one thing that runs regardless, which is another argument for the north.

Spring tides — around the new and full moon — swing hardest. The full moon falls on 22 January 2027, right at the start of your week, so expect the biggest tidal range of the month in the first few days and a gentler one by the 27th. Any Zanzibar tide chart will give you the actual times; the useful habit is to look at tomorrow's before you commit to anything tomorrow.

The two that will actually stop you

The Zanzibar insurance is not optional and your own policy does not substitute. Since 1 October 2024 every non-resident entering Zanzibar must hold a Zanzibar Insurance Corporation inbound policy — $44 for adults, covering up to 92 consecutive days — bought through the government portal at visitzanzibar.go.tz. Immigration scans the QR code at the gate. A private travel-insurance certificate, however comprehensive, is not accepted in its place. Buy it in the same sitting as the visa and keep the QR offline on your phone.

Canadians cannot get a Tanzanian visa on arrival. Canada is on the referral list alongside the UK, Australia, New Zealand, India, Nigeria and Pakistan — the eVisa must be applied for in advance at visa.immigration.go.tz. The ordinary tourist visa is $50 for 90 days and official processing is quoted at up to 10 days. Canada's own advisory still describes visa-on-arrival as available; the Tanzanian immigration portal is the one that governs at the desk. Apply a month out.

Pay in shillings

Tanzania banned foreign currency for domestic transactions — the Bank of Tanzania regulations came into force in 2024 and quoting or accepting USD for local goods and services is now an offence. In practice hotels and tour operators still quote in dollars, and government fees like the visa, the insurance and park entry are still paid in dollars online, but a restaurant bill, a taxi, a spice tour or a market stall should be settled in Tanzanian shillings.

Carry a working float in TZS from an ATM in Stone Town, keep a few clean post-2009 USD notes for anything official, and expect the enforcement to be uneven and occasionally confidently wrong in both directions. Cards work in hotels and better restaurants and almost nowhere else.

The airportAbeid Amani Karume International's Terminal 3 is new, air-conditioned and a considerable improvement on what preceded it, and it is still a small airport that gets overwhelmed when three widebodies land together. Reviews swing violently. For the noon departure on the 28th, be there at 09:00 and treat anything faster as a bonus.

Getting aroundThere is no useful public transport for visitors beyond the dala-dala minibuses, which are an experience rather than a plan. Hotels arrange transfers: roughly $10–20 airport to Stone Town, $40–60 to the north coast, $35–50 to the east. Agree the price before the car moves, in shillings.

HeatThirty degrees at 75% humidity with almost no wind inside Stone Town's alleys is more punishing than the number suggests. The local rhythm exists for a reason — out early, inside from noon to four, out again at five. Fight it and you will lose two days to a headache.

Water and powerBottled or filtered water only, including for teeth. Power cuts are routine across the island and most decent places have a generator; ask, because a room without one at 29°C overnight is a long night.
