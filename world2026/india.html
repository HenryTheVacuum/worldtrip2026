01 The time audit

Six days
is four days

IndiGo is the only carrier flying Malé to Mumbai non-stop — daily, out at 14:10 Maldives time, about three hours in the air, on the ground in Mumbai around 17:40 IST. So the 29th is an arrival evening, not a day. The 3rd is a departure. What you actually hold is four full days: 30, 31, 1 and 2 — and one of them is New Year's Eve, which is the whole argument on this page.

No jet lag to spendMalé is UTC+5, Mumbai is UTC+5:30. You land thirty minutes out of sync, which is the smallest clock change on this entire trip. Unlike Japan, nothing hands you a free 6am — if you want Mumbai before it wakes up, you have to set an alarm like an adult.

The arrival evening is genuinely usableLand 17:40, clear immigration on the e-Visa lane, and south Mumbai is 40–60 minutes away. That puts you on Marine Drive by around 20:00 on the 29th with the Queen's Necklace already lit. It is the one arrival evening on this trip worth planning something for.

1 January is a normal working dayNew Year's Day is not a gazetted national holiday in India. Monuments, trains, the ferry and the shops all run. Nothing in this window closes for the calendar — only for its own weekly closed day, which the next sections handle.

02 Two things to settle before you book

Two airports
and four days

Mumbai is in the middle of the biggest airport reshuffle in its history, and this stop's headline sight sits 350 km inland behind exactly one flight a day. Both of these change what you book, not just what you pack.

Mumbai is two airports now, and nobody has published the list

Verify the airport code on every leg

Navi Mumbai International (NMI) opened commercially on 25 December 2025. From 1 October 2026 — ten weeks before you fly — roughly 30% of the international movements currently handled at CSMIA Terminal 2 begin shifting there in phases, to free capacity while Terminal 1 is redeveloped. A second shuffle moves about a third of T1's domestic traffic into T2 from around 23–25 October.

As of September 2026 there is still no public flight-by-flight allocation. Airlines are negotiating individually and the assignment can move as schedules are finalised. Your inbound is IndiGo — which is already NMI's dominant carrier with roughly 1,110 flights a month there — so it is precisely the airline most likely to be moved.

The cost if it catches you: NMI sits about 43 km from Colaba across the Atal Setu, 50–65 minutes off-peak and 80–110 in traffic, against roughly 25 km from CSMIA. There is no inter-airport shuttle, and the Metro line that will join them isn't due until 2029. A stop that lands at one airport and leaves from the other quietly loses half a day and gains two long taxi fares.

What to doBook the Malé and Dubai legs last, and read the airport code on the ticket rather than the word "Mumbai" — BOM and NMI are different places. If they split, base yourself for the last night by the airport you leave from. The good news: Navi Mumbai was added to India's e-Visa entry points in the 10 August 2026 expansion to 85 designated points, so an e-Visa is valid at either airport. That was the version of this problem that would have ended the stop at the immigration desk.

Ajanta and Ellora cost you New Year's Eve

Straight trade — you can't have both

The caves are near Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar (the city formerly called Aurangabad, airport code still IXU). Ellora is 30 km out; Ajanta is another 102 km beyond it, two and a half hours each way. The air link is one IndiGo flight a day in each direction: out of Mumbai at 05:35 from Terminal 1, back from IXU at 07:15 into Terminal 2. There is no evening flight to soften it.

That schedule fixes the arithmetic. Both caves properly means two nights inland and two of your four full days. With the window running Tue 29 to Sun 3, the only way to fit it is to fly out on the 31st — which puts midnight on 31 December in Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar, not on Marine Drive.

The weekly closures are the one thing that cooperates. Ajanta closes Mondays, Ellora closes Tuesdays, and your window contains no Monday and only one Tuesday — arrival day, when you're in the air anyway. Every day you could actually use, both sites are open.

The middle optionEllora alone is one night and one morning: out 05:35 on the 30th, Kailasa and the Ellora groups all day, back on the 07:15 and in Mumbai by 08:15 on the 31st with New Year's Eve intact. Cave 16 — a full temple cut downward out of one piece of basalt — is the single most astonishing object on this stop, and Ajanta is the part that costs the extra day. Section 03 lays out all three shapes.

Why the recommendation keeps Ellora and drops Ajanta

They are not equivalent sites and they do not cost the same. Ellora is 45 minutes from the airport and its Cave 16, the Kailasa temple, was quarried downward out of a single hillside — roughly 200,000 tonnes of rock removed to leave a complete multi-storey temple standing in a pit. It is the kind of thing you fly across a subcontinent for, and you can be standing in it by 08:00 on the morning you land.

Ajanta is two and a half hours further, opens at 09:00, and needs three unhurried hours inside because what's there is paint, not architecture — and paint in low light, since flash is prohibited. That is a ten-hour day, and it is the day that turns one night inland into two, and one night into New Year's Eve.

The argument for taking Ajanta anyway is real and you should hear it plainly: the murals in Caves 1, 2, 16 and 17 are the oldest surviving paintings in India and there is nothing comparable anywhere. If they are why India is on this itinerary, take the Both caves shape and let the fireworks go. Do not take a shape that visits Ajanta in a hurry.

The overnight train, and why it isn't the answer

The Devagiri Express (17057) leaves Mumbai CSMT at 21:10 and reaches Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar at about 04:05; the 17058 comes back through at 23:20 and gets into CSMT at 07:10. It saves a hotel night and it is a good ride. It also arrives four hours before anything opens, and the return leg means a full extra evening inland — so it trades one early alarm for one wasted night. Take it if the flight is sold out or if the airport split has made the 05:35 impossible to reach.

Book under both names. The city was renamed Chhatrapati Sambhajinagar in 2023; the airport is still IXU and the station code is still AWB, and half the booking sites still say Aurangabad.

06 The rock-cut question

Three sets
of caves

This stop is, whether or not you planned it that way, a rock-cut architecture trip. Three sites, twelve centuries of chisel work, and a clean split in what each one is for — which is the useful way to choose between them when you only have room for some.

The one arithmetic that decides it

Ellora is 30 km / 45 minutes from the IXU terminal. Land at 06:40 on the 05:35 flight, be at the gate by 07:45, and you have the Kailasa courtyard more or less to yourself before the first coach from Mumbai arrives. Ajanta is 102 km / 2h 30m further and doesn't open until 09:00, so the earliest useful start is 06:30 from the city — and you are back after dark.

That is the entire difference between one night inland and two, and therefore between keeping New Year's Eve and losing it. Everything else about the two sites is a matter of taste.

Practicalities at the gates

Carry cash. ASI ticket counters and the shuttle buses at both cave sites take rupees and the card machines are frequently down. ₹3,000–5,000 in small notes covers tickets, shuttles, guides and lunch for the whole inland trip.

Hire the car, not the tour. A car and driver for a full Ellora day runs roughly ₹3,000–4,000 and for the longer Ajanta day around ₹4,500 — book through the hotel the night before. Group coach tours leave late and leave the caves at the worst hour.

Shoes off, repeatedly. Both sites are live-enough religious spaces that you will remove footwear many times. Slip-ons, and socks you don't mind on warm stone.

07 Why this window works

The dry
half

Mumbai's year is not divided into seasons so much as into monsoon and not-monsoon. June through September delivers over two metres of rain onto the city; late December delivers, on an average year, essentially none. The itinerary's reason for putting India here is sound and the chart is the proof.

Indicative long-term monthly normals · individual years vary widely, particularly at the monsoon's edges

What it actually feels like28–32°C by day, 18–22°C after dark, humidity down around 55–65% from the summer's 85–95%. That is warm rather than oppressive, and the sea breeze along Marine Drive after sunset is the reason half the city walks there in the evening. Cotton, one layer for the late evening, and shoes you can do twenty kilometres a day in.

The catch is the airMumbai's winter AQI typically runs 80–150 — moderate to unhealthy for sensitive groups. It is nothing like the winter smog inland, but the same still air that keeps the rain away also keeps the particulates in. If you're sensitive, carry an N95 and check the reading before committing to a long walking day.

Crowds and pricesLate December is Mumbai's peak. Hotel rates run 30–50% above baseline across your window and worst on the 31st itself; mid-November is roughly the last sensible time to book. Bandra's Christmas lights on Chapel Road and Hill Road stay up into early January, which is a free and genuinely lovely thing that only this window gets you.

How to actually pay for things

UPI is how India pays. Foreign visitors can now get a UPI One World prepaid wallet — KYC with your passport and visa through a partner app, load it in rupees, and then scan the QR code stuck to the counter like everyone else. It covers the auto-rickshaw, the chai stall and the kirana shop, none of which want your Visa card.

But carry cash anyway. ASI gates, cave shuttles, ferry counters and temple donation boxes are cash-first and their card terminals fail often. ₹3,000–5,000 in small notes. ATMs are everywhere in Mumbai and sparse at the caves.

Cards work in the places you'd expect — hotels, restaurants, malls, Uber. Nowhere else reliably.

The ferry queueElephanta tickets are sold at the Gateway, not online, and the counter queue runs 30–45 minutes on weekends and holidays — which your whole window is. Be there for the 09:00 boat and you skip most of it; arrive at 11:00 and you'll spend the wait standing in the sun.

TippingRound up for autos and taxis, 5–10% at restaurants that haven't already added a service charge, ₹100–200 for a cave guide who was actually good. Nothing at all is expected at ASI gates or on the ferry.

The Dharavi questionDharavi tours are genuinely contested. The better operators are Dharavi-run, put most of the fee back into local programmes and ban photography of residents; the worse ones are voyeurism with a minibus. If you go, choose on those three criteria or don't go.
