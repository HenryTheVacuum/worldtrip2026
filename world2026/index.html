The jumpFlying west over the Pacific a calendar day disappears at the date line. You spend the next four months winning it back, an hour at a time.

The odd offsetsThree stops refuse whole hours: Nepal runs UTC+5:45, India UTC+5:30, and Bhutan a tidy UTC+6 between them — so the Himalayan leg alone shifts your watch by quarter-hours.

The ticks upWestbound doesn't always mean backward: Malaysia (+8), Tanzania (+3) and Morocco (+1) each nudge the clock forward again before the long slide resumes.

02 The route

Always west,
always dry

One rule shapes the trip: never land in a rainy season. The heading is broadly westward, with a deliberate loop back east through Southeast Asia to catch it at its driest. Starting mid-October threads the needle — East Asia after typhoons, Southeast Asia as the monsoon ends, South Asia and the Gulf in their winter prime, East Africa inside its short dry window, and Morocco before the heat. What got cut, the rain mostly cut for us: Madagascar, Victoria Falls, Botswana and Namibia are all wet from November to March. Bali is the deliberate exception — added knowing December is its wet season. Tap any waypoint for its dossier.

Two that need real lead time

Bhutan must be arranged through a licensed operator with the daily Sustainable Development Fee paid up front — start that 6–8 weeks out. China still requires a full tourist visa for Canadians, with an embassy or visa-centre application and biometrics in person — allow 3–4 weeks and apply before you leave.

Everything else is visa-free, on-arrival, or an e-visa you can do from a hostel bed. Rules change constantly — verify each line against the destination government's own site before booking. Passport: valid to at least September 2027, with plenty of blank pages — this trip uses a lot of stamps.
