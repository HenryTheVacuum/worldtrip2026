01 The time audit

Six days
is four days

You arrive from Luang Prabang on an afternoon flight — the direct LPQ–SAI service leaves around 14:45 and runs about 1h25 — then lose an hour on the road, because the new Siem Reap airport is 40 km out of town. Departure day goes to the airport run at the other end. What's left is four full days and two fragments, and every plan below is built against four.

No jet lag at allLaos and Cambodia are both UTC+7. You land on the same body clock you left with, which is the first time on this trip that's been true. Bank it: the 5am starts that Angkor rewards will cost you nothing but willpower.

The airport is the taxSiem Reap–Angkor International sits 40 km southeast of town — 45 to 55 minutes each way. Fixed-price taxi is $35–40, Grab runs $25–35, and most hotels will send a car for somewhere between free and $25. Arrange the pickup before you fly; the tuk-tuks that used to meet the old airport are not a realistic option over that distance.

Sunrise is 06:18Mid-December sunrise in Siem Reap lands between 06:12 and 06:27, sunset between 17:35 and 17:48. Eleven and a half hours of daylight, and the good half is the first three. Park gates open at 05:00.

02 Three things that changed

Your guidebook
is out of date

Cambodia has moved further in three years than anywhere else on this itinerary — two of its airports were replaced, its land border with Thailand shut, and its visitor numbers fell off a cliff. All three matter to this leg, and two of them are good news.

REP and PNH are both gone

Check your bookings

Siem Reap–Angkor International (SAI) opened on 16 October 2023 and the old REP airport closed. Phnom Penh's Techo International (KTI) began operating 9 September 2025 and took over as the capital's hub; the old PNH is now a military field with a little domestic traffic.

Both new airports are much further out than the ones they replaced — SAI is 40 km from Siem Reap, KTI is 19 km south of Phnom Penh. Old blog posts, older guidebooks and some booking engines still show the retired codes.

Do thisWhen you book the LPQ inbound and the KUL outbound, confirm the codes read SAI and KTI, not REP and PNH. Then budget an hour each way for the transfer instead of the twenty minutes the old airports needed.

The Thai land border is closed

Doesn't touch you — but know it

Thailand shut its Cambodian crossings in late June 2025 after armed clashes over disputed border temples. Two ceasefires — July and December 2025 — did not hold; the December agreement broke down in early January 2026, and Poipet–Aranyaprathet, Cham Yeam–Hat Lek and O'Smach–Chong Jom have stayed closed to travellers through 2026 with no reopening date announced.

Canada's advisory currently says avoid all travel within 50 km of the Thai border, and Cambodia overall sits at "exercise a high degree of caution." Flights between the two countries run normally.

What it rules outPreah Vihear, Banteay Chhmar and the overland hop to Bangkok. None of these were in your plan — you fly in from Laos and out to Malaysia — but if anyone suggests a "quick trip up to the border temple," the answer is no.

Angkor is a third emptier

Unambiguously good for you

Angkor drew 359,471 international visitors in the first five months of 2026, down 31.8% year on year, with ticket revenue of US$17.2 million, off 30%. Cambodian analysts blame the Thai border conflict, the regional scam-centre reputation and a broader economic slowdown.

December is still the busiest month of the Cambodian year — school holidays plus the best weather — and daily ticket sales in peak season have historically run 5,000 to 7,000. You will not have Angkor Wat to yourself at sunrise. You will have noticeably more of it than someone who came in December 2024.

The lever is still the clockCrowd density at Angkor is driven far more by hour than by month. See the crowd clock in section 06 — the difference between 07:30 and 11:30 is bigger than the difference between December and August.

03 The open question

Phnom Penh:
in or out

Angkor is fixed and it is why this stop exists. Phnom Penh is 320 km away down a two-lane national highway — there is no expressway; the Phnom Penh–Siem Reap route is still only at feasibility study — so putting the capital in costs you either half a day on a bus or a short flight on an airline with a reputation for schedule changes. Three shapes fit the same 11–16 Dec window.

Recommended

Both, by air

Siem Reap ×3→ ✈ 50min→ Phnom Penh ×2→ KTI

Two and a half temple days, then a midday hop south on the 14th. Air Cambodia flies SAI–KTI twice daily, about 50 minutes, $80–200 one way. Door to door it is still three to four hours once you count two long airport transfers, but it saves you roughly half a day against the bus.

Why the capital earns its two nights: Tuol Sleng and Choeung Ek are not a sightseeing stop, they are the reason the rest of Cambodia looks the way it does. Doing them on a rushed morning before a flight is the wrong way to do them. Two nights also means the outbound to Kuala Lumpur leaves from KTI, which has far more KUL service than Siem Reap does.

The one thing all three have in common

Whichever shape you pick, the 3-day Angkor pass is the right ticket — even if you only end up using two of its days. At $62 it undercuts two separate $37 day passes by $12, and it is valid on any three days inside a ten-day window, so the days need not be consecutive. Section 07 has the arithmetic if you want to see it move.

What's cut in every version

Preah Vihear. The cliff-top temple on the Thai frontier is the disputed site the 2025 fighting was about, and it sits inside the 50 km avoid-all-travel zone. Not this trip, and not soon.

Sihanoukville and the islands. Six hours south of Phnom Penh, and the town has been hollowed out by the casino-and-compound economy. You have the Philippines and Malaysia for beaches.

Battambang. Colonial shophouses, the bamboo train, genuinely good food, and three hours out of Siem Reap in the wrong direction. It needs an overnight you don't have.

Kampot and Kep. Pepper farms, river sunsets, crab. Lovely, and a full day each way from anywhere you'll be.

06 The crowd clock

Hour beats
month

December is Angkor's busiest month and it barely matters, because the crowd at any given temple is a function of the coach timetable. Buses load after hotel breakfast and unload between nine and eleven; they go back for lunch and return for sunset. There are two quiet windows a day and they are enormous.

Window one: 06:45–08:30The sunrise crowd is a specific crowd. It comes for the reflecting pool, it photographs the sun clearing the towers, and then a very large fraction of it goes back to a hotel for breakfast. If you stay in the park through that exodus you get the best ninety minutes of the day — Angkor Wat's interior galleries almost to yourself, in low gold light, before the coaches land.

Window two: 15:30–17:30The genuinely underrated one. The heat has broken, the coaches have done their morning circuit, and the light going into sunset is better than the light coming out of it. Ta Prohm and Preah Khan in late afternoon are a different experience from Ta Prohm at eleven.

The one to avoid11:00 to 14:00 is peak coach traffic and peak heat simultaneously. Do not spend it at a temple. Spend it eating, sleeping, or at the Angkor National Museum, which is air-conditioned and largely empty at exactly that hour.

Sunrise: the reflecting pool, or not

The standard shot is from the north reflecting pool, and in peak season several thousand people want it. Two alternatives are worth knowing. The south pool gives the same reflection with a fraction of the crowd. And staying outside the moat, on the western causeway approach, lets you watch the whole temple go from silhouette to gold with nobody in front of you — a better experience, a worse photograph.

If you would rather skip the scrum entirely: Srah Srang, the royal bathing pool three kilometres east, has water, sky, a laterite terrace and almost nobody. Pre Rup, five kilometres east, gives you a 360° temple-mountain view over the forest. Both are inside the park and covered by your pass.

Dress code, enforced

Shoulders and knees covered is the rule across the park and it is checked at the stairs to Angkor Wat's upper level (the Bakan), where guards turn people away. Vests, short shorts and see-through fabric will fail. Lightweight long trousers and a light long-sleeved layer are also the right call for the sun regardless.

The Bakan has a daily visitor cap and closes on Buddhist holy days; Phnom Bakheng caps sunset at 300 people. Neither is bookable in advance — you queue or you go early.

07 The ticket maths

Buy the

Angkor passes come in three sizes and only one of them makes sense for a stop this length. Tick what you actually plan to do and watch the numbers move. Prices are set by Angkor Enterprise and are the same everywhere — the official ticket office on Apsara Road, the online portal, the Heritage Walk kiosks and the counter at SAI arrivals all charge identical amounts.

Three rules that save money or time

A 1-day pass bought after 17:00 is valid the following day. Historically this also let you into the park that same evening for sunset — treat the free sunset as a bonus that may or may not still be honoured, and buy on the strength of the next-day validity alone.

Not every temple is on the pass. Angkor Wat, Bayon, Ta Prohm, Preah Khan, Banteay Srei and Beng Mealea are included. Phnom Kulen ($20) and Koh Ker ($15) are separate tickets, as are Preah Vihear and Banteay Chhmar — the last two being in the border zone you're avoiding anyway.

Buy online the night before. The portal runs 24 hours; the physical office opens at 04:30 for sunrise buyers and the queue at 05:00 in December is real. Children under 12 are free on a passport, no pass issued.

08 Why this window

The best six
days of the year

Mid-December is not a compromise here — it is Cambodia at its objective best. The northeast monsoon has cleared the sky, the wet-season heat has broken, and the ground is dry enough that the unpaved temple approaches don't turn to red soup. Expect 29–30°C by day, 22°C at night, 65% humidity and roughly one rainy day in the month.

The one trade-offDry season means the Tonlé Sap is falling. From roughly August to November the lake floods to several times its dry-season area and the stilt villages sit in open water with paddleable flooded forest; by mid-December the water is receding and the houses stand tall on their poles above mud channels. It's still worth seeing — it just isn't the postcard version.

No holiday clashesWater Festival, Cambodia's biggest, fell on 23 November 2026 — three weeks before you land. Independence Day is 9 November, Peace Day is 29 December. Nothing between 11 and 16 December 2026 is a public holiday, so museums, offices and the ticket portal all run normal hours.

Peak season pricingDecember is high season for accommodation in both cities, and the good boutique places in Siem Reap's Wat Bo and Kandal Village areas fill. Book rooms now; almost nothing else here needs advance purchase.

Where to stay in Siem ReapWat Bo, across the river, and Kandal Village to the west of the old French quarter are both quiet, walkable and full of good small hotels. Pub Street is loud until two in the morning and you are getting up at five. Anywhere in town is 20–30 minutes from the park gate by tuk-tuk.

Where to stay in Phnom PenhRiverside near Sisowath Quay is the obvious choice and works fine; BKK1 is greener, calmer and has the better restaurants. Both are 30–40 minutes from Techo airport by road, longer in traffic — the airport express bus runs 05:30 to 23:30.

EatingAmok trey, fish steamed in banana leaf with kroeung; num banh chok, the rice-noodle breakfast soup sold from carts before eight; kuy teav noodle soup; Kampot pepper on everything. Cambodian food is quieter than Thai and more herbal than Vietnamese, and it is badly underrated. The best of it is street-side and costs a couple of dollars.

Scam-centre countryCambodia's reputation for online fraud compounds is real and has drawn US sanctions, but it is an industry that preys on people recruited into it, not on tourists at temples. It affects you in one way only: be sceptical of anything that arrives unsolicited offering work, romance or investment, and give Bavet and the Sihanoukville casino strip a miss.
