01 The time audit

The most
generous week

After twenty-three stops of three and four-night sprints, this is the one with room in it. Seven nights, one base, no internal moves, and the best weather on the entire itinerary. You land on the evening of the 28th and the outbound flight is an evening departure too, which means the 4th stays usable — so the real budget is six full days plus two workable fragments.

No jet lag to speak ofZanzibar is UTC+3 and Cape Town is UTC+2. You lose an hour. After the thirteen-hour whiplash of the Tokyo arrival and the long grind up the Gulf, this stop asks nothing of your body clock at all — which is just as well, because the days here are physical ones.

The weather sets the order, not the calendarAlmost everything worth doing in Cape Town is outdoors and at altitude or at sea. Table Mountain, Cape Point, Lion's Head and the Robben Island ferry are all cancellable by wind. Do not build a fixed schedule. Build a list, check the mountain every morning, and spend the clear days high.

Build in a float dayWith six full days you can afford to hold one — 2 February in the plan below — entirely in reserve. Something will blow out. When the south-easter runs for two days straight, which it does in February, that reserve is the difference between seeing the mountain and not.

02 Two things that don't move

Both ends
are broken

The stop itself is the easiest on the itinerary. The flights either side of it are the hardest. Both were found in research, both are fixable, and both are cheaper to fix now than in January.

Getting in: Thursday has no nonstop

Filed separately · fix is straightforward

Airlink's Cape Town–Zanzibar nonstop, launched 3 October 2026, is the only nonstop on the route and it operates once a week, on Saturdays — 4Z357, ZNZ 15:30 → CPT 20:50. The handoff date is Thursday 28 January. There is no flight.

Slipping to the Saturday costs two of Cape Town's seven days, which is the wrong trade on the stop with the most to do and the most weather risk.

The fixAirlink 4Z11, ZNZ 12:00 → JNB 14:55, operates Mondays and Thursdays — the 28th is a scheduled day. Connect onto the Johannesburg–Cape Town shuttle (Airlink, FlySafair, SAA and Lift all fly it many times daily) and you are in Cape Town mid-evening on the 28th, date unchanged. Buy it on one PNR; allow three hours at JNB, because it is an international-to-domestic transfer with immigration and a bag re-check. Avoid the Nairobi and Addis routings — both hubs sit on South Africa's yellow-fever list and a long delay there creates an entry problem at Cape Town that cannot be solved airside.

Getting out: there is no route to Praia

Worth restructuring the itinerary for

Cape Town to Praia is 7,037 km and there is no nonstop and no one-stop. Praia's only intercontinental gateways are Lisbon, Casablanca, Dakar and Bissau; Cape Town flies to none of them, and TAP's Johannesburg–Lisbon nonstop has been dead since March 2025.

Every workable routing runs north to Europe, then back south-west: three flights, roughly 30–32 hours. Leave on the evening of the 4th and you reach Praia in the small hours of the 6th. Cape Verde has four nights; two of them go to the journey.

The fixSwap stops 25 and 26 — Cape Town → Morocco → Cape Verde → Lisbon. Casablanca–Praia is a 3h50 Royal Air Maroc nonstop five days a week, and Praia–Lisbon is TAP once or twice daily. Every leg becomes nonstop or a single connection, the double backtrack disappears, and nothing is lost seasonally — both countries are dry and mild across the whole 4–14 February span. Full working in flag-capetown-praia-no-route; section 03 below lays out the alternative.

The window itself is perfect

Nothing to fix

February is the Cape's driest and most settled month — 8 mm of rain across the month against 85 mm in July, an average high of 28.4°C, and 4.6 rainy days, the fewest of any month in the year. Eleven hours of sunshine a day.

You also arrive after the South African school holidays, which end in mid-January. Late January and February are the sweet spot: summer weather without the domestic crush that fills every Camps Bay restaurant between Christmas and the second week of January.

The catch, such as it isFebruary is also peak south-easter. The Cape Doctor blows October to March, builds through the afternoon, and closes the cableway above roughly 60 km/h at the summit. It is a scheduling problem, not a weather problem — see section 06.

Cape Town to Casablanca is one European connection — CDG–CMN runs several times daily on Royal Air Maroc and Air France, so an overnight out of Cape Town puts you in Morocco the following afternoon. Then Casablanca–Praia is a 3h50 nonstop: AT591 Tuesdays, Fridays and Sundays, AT595 Thursdays and Saturdays, both at 21:30. Praia–Lisbon is TAP, one or two daily, 4h30, straight into the final stop.

Suggested dates: Morocco 4–10 Feb, Cape Verde 10–14 Feb, Lisbon from the 14th as written. A 10 February Praia arrival needs a Tuesday departure from Casablanca — AT591 flies Tuesdays, so it lands. The 11th and 13th also fall on operating days if the Morocco leg wants a day more or less.

Depart Cape Town on the evening of the 4th, cross overnight to London, Paris or Amsterdam, connect to Lisbon, and take TAP's 20:50 Lisbon–Praia. Arrival is around 00:20 on the 6th. Cape Verde becomes an effective 6–8 February — two nights, not four — on an island stop whose entire point is slowing down. Then you fly back north-east to Casablanca and north again to Lisbon, crossing your own path twice.

The one thing in its favour: the Cape Town week is untouched. Leaving a day early to recover a Cape Verde night is the worse version of this — Cape Town is the weather-dependent stop and its last day is the weather reserve.

Why there is no third option

Dakar. Praia–Dakar is a busy short hop — around 29 flights a month — and on a map it is the obvious way in from the south. Cape Town has no service to Dakar and no one-stop worth the name. Dead end.

Sal instead of Praia. Sal has more European charter and scheduled traffic than Praia does, and inter-island hops to Praia are frequent. It does not help: you still have to reach Europe first, so it adds a leg rather than removing one.

A Southern African hub. Johannesburg used to be the answer — TAP flew it to Lisbon nonstop. That route's last scheduled flight was 24 March 2025. Royal Air Maroc does not serve Johannesburg or Cape Town either. There is currently no African-side shortcut at all.

06 The mountain and the wind

Go up on
the first
clear morning

Table Mountain is the reason the week has a float day. The cableway closes for wind — above roughly 60 km/h at the summit, or gusts past 90 — and February is peak south-easter season. The wind is also diurnal: it builds through the day and peaks mid-to-late afternoon. So the rule is simple and unromantic. Whichever morning is calm and clear, go up that morning, whatever else was on the list.

Two ways upThe cableway is R475 return online, R530 at the ticket office, for the year from 1 July 2026. Buy online the morning you go — tickets are valid seven days, so a closure does not burn them. The walk up is Platteklip Gorge: about two hours, relentlessly steep, no shade, no water. Start at first light or don't start. Many people walk up and ride down, which is the right way round and costs R300 one-way.

Check before you leave the hotelThe cableway publishes its operating status each morning and it changes fast. A clear sky at sea level tells you nothing — the tablecloth cloud forms on the summit in a south-easter while Sea Point is in full sun. If it is shut and the sky is good, Lion's Head is the substitute: 45 minutes up, chains and ladders near the top, and arguably a better view because Table Mountain is in it.

Hiking safety is a real thingCanada's own advisory notes attacks on hikers on both Table Mountain and Lion's Head. Go in a group, stay on the main routes, go in daylight, and don't take a phone out on an empty path. The full-moon Lion's Head walk is popular partly because the numbers make it safe.

Fire seasonLate summer is when the Cape burns. Fynbos needs fire and the mountain has large ones most years — the 2025 blaze closed parts of the park. If a fire is running, trails and sometimes the cableway shut with no notice. It is another argument for taking the first good morning rather than the convenient one.

What isn't on the list

Food and wine. The single best thing about this stop financially. A very good restaurant dinner costs a fraction of its Tokyo or Dubai equivalent, and the wine on the list is world-class and local. Cellar-door tastings run R60 to R260 a person and are frequently waived if you buy a bottle.

Getting around. Uber and Bolt both work well and are cheap in Cape Town — for the City Bowl, Sea Point and the Waterfront you do not need a car at all. Car hire is for the peninsula and the Winelands, and those are the only two days that need it.

The Wild Card. If the tally above clears roughly R1,500 of SANParks gates, look at an international Wild Card cluster for the Cape parks before you buy day tickets. It is a marginal call on one week; do the sum at booking with the then-current tariff.

Where to stayOne base for seven nights. The City Bowl — Gardens, Tamboerskloof, Oranjezicht — puts you walking distance from the restaurants and ten minutes from the cableway road, and it is the right answer. Sea Point is flatter, breezier and good for the promenade. Camps Bay is beautiful, expensive and inconvenient to everything except its own beach. The V&A Waterfront is safe, sterile and where the Robben Island ferry leaves from.

The two-tier gateAlmost every park and museum charges international visitors several times the South African rate — Kirstenbosch is R270 against R110, Cape Point R515 against a local fraction. This is normal, it is not a scam, and arguing about it at the gate marks you out. Budget for it and move on.

HarvestYou arrive in the middle of vintage. Late January through February is when the Winelands actually work — trucks of grapes on the roads, cellars open and loud, and estates running harvest events. It is the best possible month to be there and the worst for spontaneous cellar tours, since everyone is busy. Book tastings a day or two ahead.

The wind, once moreIf the south-easter is running, the Atlantic beaches — Camps Bay, Clifton, Llandudno — are unusable, and False Bay on the other side of the peninsula is calm. Muizenberg, Fish Hoek and Kalk Bay are the wind day answer, and the water there is ten degrees warmer besides.
