01 The time audit

Three days
is three days

For once the calendar tells the truth. Korea sits in the same time zone as Japan, the flight is under two hours, and there is no date line to pay. You land on the evening of the 22nd, you leave on the morning of the 26th, and the three days in between are genuinely three days. Everything that made the Japan week arithmetically hostile is absent here. Something else takes its place.

The jet-lag gift expires hereJapan's whole strategy was 6am. That was a loan against a thirteen-hour time shift, and by the 23rd you are into your second week on Tokyo time with nothing left to borrow. Korea is the same clock. You will wake at a normal hour and want to stay up, which is exactly the opposite of what stop 01 trained you for.

Seoul is an evening city anywayThis turns out not to matter, because Seoul does not reward dawn the way Japan does. The palaces open at 09:00. Bukchon is legally closed to visitors until 10:00. The markets, the alleys and the mountain-view bars are all at their best after dark. Adjust the shape of the day rather than fighting it.

The arrival evening is half a thingLand around 17:00, clear a digital immigration desk, be at a hotel by 19:00. That's dinner and a walk, not a plan. But unlike Tokyo you won't be destroyed — it's a two-hour hop, not a Pacific crossing. Cheonggyecheon at night is ten minutes from most central hotels and costs nothing.

One correction carried overThe Japan page has its weekdays a day out — 22 Oct 2026 is a Thursday, not a Wednesday, and 16 Oct is a Friday. Everything here is built on the corrected calendar, which matters more than it sounds: your three days fall on Friday, Saturday and Sunday, several sites close on Mondays, and the one border train runs Fridays only.

02 Three things that don't move

Fixed before
you choose

These hold whatever you decide about leaving the city. Two of them are the reason the day-by-day is ordered the way it is.

The colour finally shows up

Partial, but real

Japan gave you a hard zero. Korea gives you the first autumn of the trip — but on the same terms Japan set, which is to say by altitude. Seoraksan, at 1,708 m in the north-east, is at or very near peak during your exact window. Bukhansan's upper ridge, 836 m and inside Seoul's city limits, is just beginning to turn.

What is not ready is the postcard: the ginkgo avenues, the palace maples, Namsan. Seoul's street trees peak in the first half of November, a good two weeks after you leave.

The playGo up. Bukhansan's ridge is roughly a week ahead of the streets below it and is reachable on a subway ticket. That is the entire trick and it is covered in section 06.

Your three days are Fri, Sat, Sun

The worst possible draw

Korea hikes. On an autumn weekend the popular Bukhansan trails carry queues at the scrambling sections, and the summit of Baegundae can involve genuinely waiting your turn on fixed ropes. Markets, palaces and Bukchon all take a weekend surcharge in bodies too.

You get exactly one weekday, and it is Friday. Whatever you most want uncrowded has to go there.

The fixPut the mountain on Friday and the city on the weekend. Seoul absorbs crowds; a granite ridge does not. If you take the DMZ option instead, Friday goes to the border and the hike takes the weekend hit — that's the real cost of that choice, and it's priced into section 03.

Bukchon is closed before 10:00

₩100,000 fine

Jongno-gu made the residential core of Bukchon Hanok Village a Special Management Area. In the Red Zone — the famous alley, Bukchon-ro 11-gil, and its neighbours — visitors are barred from 17:00 to 10:00 the following morning, and the penalty is a hundred thousand won. Enforcement with fines has been live since March 2025 and stewards patrol the lanes from late afternoon.

Separately, since 1 January 2026 chartered tour buses are banned across a 2.3 km stretch of Bukchon-ro at all hours. Only relevant if you book a private car.

What this costs youThe single most photographed hanok street in Korea is available in a seven-hour window, in the middle of the day, in the worst light. Take the 10:00 opening, keep your voice down, and get your quiet shots in Seochon or Ikseon-dong instead — neither is restricted.

03 The open question

Leave the city:
yes or no

With one base and three days there is no routing puzzle — there is one decision, and it is whether any part of this stop happens outside Seoul. The honest answer is that almost nothing within reach earns a whole day. The DMZ is the one exception worth arguing about, and only in its half-day form.

Recommended

All Seoul

Mountain ×1→ Old city ×1→ River & west ×1→ ICN

Three days, three distinct Seouls, no coach and no checkpoint. Friday goes up Bukhansan while the trails are empty and the ridge has just turned. Saturday is the Joseon city — palaces, the Secret Garden, Jongmyo, Bukchon inside its legal window. Sunday is the modern one: the Han, the silver grass at Haneul Park, and a wall walk at dusk.

What you're actually buying is depth in a city that rewards it. Seoul is not a checklist town; the good parts are alleys, meals and hills, and all three of those want unhurried hours rather than a longer list of sights.

What the DMZ actually is in 2026, before you book it

The JSA at Panmunjom — the blue huts, the facing guards, the line you step over — has been shut to ordinary visitors since July 2023, after a US soldier crossed during a tour. A partial reopening in summer 2025 restored access to the JSA Visitor Centre on selected tours, but the conference buildings themselves remain closed and the orientation tour was redesigned to remove them. Many tours still sold as "JSA tours" substitute an indoor JSA Experience Museum. If that distinction matters to you, ask the operator point-blank what you will actually stand in front of.

What a standard DMZ tour reliably delivers is the Third Tunnel, Dora Observatory looking north, and Imjingak. It is worth doing, it is strange and sober, and it is not the photograph you have in your head. Bring your physical passport — the manifest is submitted to the military in advance and a photo of your passport will not get you past the checkpoint.

One curiosity for your dates: the Seoul–Dorasan DMZ Peace Connection Train resumed in April 2026 after a six-year suspension and runs on Fridays, with about 120 seats. Friday 23 October is the only day of this stop it could apply to. Availability is thin and it does not replace a tour, but it exists.

What's cut either way, and why

Seoraksan. Painful, because it is the one place in Korea at genuine peak colour during your window. It is also two and a half hours each way by express bus to Sokcho, plus a park transfer, and the cable car sells only on the day. As a day trip in peak week you would spend nine hours moving for maybe three on the mountain, in Korea's heaviest domestic travel weekend of the autumn. As an overnight it costs two of your three days. Bank it.

Suwon Hwaseong. Forty minutes on Line 1 and a genuinely superb 18th-century fortress wall you can walk end to end. The best-value day trip on the board — and still a poor trade against a third day in Seoul on a first visit. It also sits outside the Climate Card boundary, which is a small tax on top.

Nami Island and the Garden of Morning Calm. Ninety minutes out, at their best in the first week of November, and heavily coached. You would arrive a week early to a car park.

Gyeongju, Jeonju, Busan. All two hours or more by KTX, all worth two nights, none worth one day. These are the reason to come back, not the reason to leave Seoul now.

06 The colour question

Go up, not
out

This is the same chart as Japan's, one week later and one country west, and it has the same shape for the same reason: autumn in East Asia is a function of altitude before it is a function of the calendar. Korea's colour starts on Seoraksan's granite in late September and walks downhill and southward for eight weeks. Your window catches the top of that staircase, not the bottom.

Why Bukhansan is the answer and Seoraksan isn't

Bukhansan rises to 836 m on the northern edge of the metropolitan area and is inside Seoul's city limits — Line 3 to Gupabal, then a bus to the trailhead, about fifty minutes door to trail. Its ridge typically colours from around the end of October, roughly a week ahead of the street trees below. You will not get peak. You will get the first proper reds and golds of the whole trip, on granite, with twenty million people laid out beneath you.

Seoraksan is at peak. It is also 2h30 by express bus to Sokcho and a transfer beyond that, and its long-run normal peak is 17 October — meaning it peaked on the 29th in 2024 and the 24th in 2025 and nobody can tell you in advance which kind of year 2026 is. Chasing a peak that mobile, at that distance, with three days, is how people lose a day and see a car park.

The consolation is that the two mountains are the same phenomenon at different heights, and one of them is a subway ride away.

Colour inside the city, for the days you're not on a mountain

Changdeokgung's Secret Garden is the best of it — a 45-minute guided walk through pond pavilions under maples, on ground that was closed to commoners for four centuries. Entry is capped and timed. Book it online through the Royal Palaces and Tombs office, or take one of the roughly fifty tickets released on the day at the gate and accept the risk.

Deoksugung's stone wall path — Doldam-gil — is the ginkgo walk everyone photographs, and it is genuinely lovely in the evening because the palace itself stays open late. It will be more green than gold on your dates.

Haneul Park is the outlier that doesn't depend on maples at all: a landfill turned meadow above the Han, planted with silver grass that peaks in exactly your window and runs an evening-lit festival in the second half of October. Two hundred and ninety-one steps up, or a short shuttle. This one you have timed perfectly.

The 75-minute lineUnder it, you can be back for an afternoon and the trip is an addition. Over it, you have committed the day, and on a three-day stop the day you commit is a third of everything. Only the DMZ sits close enough to the line to argue about, and only in its half-day form.

Suwon is the near missForty minutes on Line 1 to a UNESCO fortress wall you can walk in three hours, with an excellent local speciality — galbi — waiting at the end. On a five-day Seoul stop it goes straight in. On three, the third Seoul day beats it, and it isn't close.

Nothing here is a mistake to skipGyeongju is the old Silla capital and deserves two nights. Jeonju is the best eating city in the country. Busan is a different Korea entirely. All three are reasons to plan a return, and none of them survives contact with a 24-hour budget.

08 The transit maths

Buy the
Climate Card

The opposite verdict to Japan's, for the opposite reason. Seoul's Climate Card is an unlimited subway-and-bus pass sold in short-term tourist durations — ₩5,000 for one day, ₩10,000 for three, ₩15,000 for five, plus ₩3,000 once for the physical card. Against a ₩1,550 base fare it breaks even at roughly four rides a day, and visitors typically take five or six. Tick the legs you'll ride and watch it pay for itself — then read the two traps underneath, because both are real and neither is obvious.

Trap one: the clock starts when you charge it, not when you ride

Short-term passes count calendar days from the moment they are loaded, not from first use. Charge a three-day pass at the airport on Thursday evening and it dies on Saturday night, stranding your Sunday. Your stop spans five calendar days of transit — the 22nd through the 26th — so either buy the five-day pass, or pay cash on the arrival evening and load a three-day pass on Friday morning, which covers Friday, Saturday and Sunday and leaves only the Monday airport run outside.

A second wrinkle: the mobile version lives in the Tmoney app and needs Android 12 or later. iPhones cannot use it at all, because Apple doesn't open NFC to third parties in Korea. Buy the physical card. Since March 2026 the newer machines at over 270 stations on Lines 1–8 take foreign credit cards, which removes the old cash-only annoyance, but stock at convenience stores is genuinely patchy — get it at a station.

Trap two: it stops at the city line

The Climate Card is a Seoul pass. It does not cover the AREX from Incheon in either direction, red express buses, the Shinbundang line, or any journey that begins or ends in Gyeonggi-do — which rules out Suwon, and rules out getting yourself to a DMZ tour meeting point outside the boundary. Tap out at a Gyeonggi station and the gate will simply refuse you and a member of staff will take the full fare in cash.

So roughly ₩22,000 of your Seoul transport — the two airport runs — sits outside any pass no matter what you buy. That is not an argument against the card; it's the reason the arithmetic below only counts the legs the card can actually pay for. Prices and boundaries both move — check before buying.

Getting in from Incheon

AREX Express runs non-stop to Seoul Station in 43 minutes with a reserved seat and luggage space, priced somewhere between ₩9,500 and ₩13,000 depending on whether you buy online, at the machine or at the counter — quoted rates vary widely, so check rather than assume. AREX All-Stop does the same run in about an hour for around ₩5,000 on a T-money tap, and it stops at Hongdae and Digital Media City, which is often more useful than Seoul Station.

Airport limousine buses run ₩17,000–18,000 direct to hotel clusters — worth it for Gangnam, pointless for anywhere on Line 1 or 4. A taxi is ₩65,000–85,000; hail it through Kakao T rather than the rank so you get the fare estimate up front. The last Express leaves T1 around 22:40, so a 17:00 landing has no timing problem at all.

WeatherMaterially colder than the Japan leg. Expect 17–19°C by day and 7–10°C overnight, against Hiroshima's low twenties. Clear, dry and bright — late October is the best weather Korea gets all year. What your Japan packing won't cover is a granite ridge at 09:00, which will be near freezing in wind. Add one warm layer and gloves you can lose.

Two festivals worth checkingThe Jogyesa Chrysanthemum Festival fills the temple courtyard near Anguk with flower sculptures through roughly the second half of October, free, five minutes from Insadong. The Seoul Silver Grass Festival at Haneul Park runs mid-to-late October with evening lighting. Both are annual and both publish dates late — confirm before building an evening around either.

EatingThe pattern from Japan holds: the best meals will be cheap and served at a counter. Where Korea differs is that dinner is social and long, and a lot of the good stuff is minimum-two-people — barbecue, jjigae, whole braised dishes. Solo travellers do better at gimbap counters, noodle shops and market stalls, all of which are excellent.

DrinkingEuljiro's nogari alley, the Ikseon-dong lanes and the Hongdae backstreets are three completely different evenings. Soju is cheap enough to be a hazard; makgeolli with a pancake on a cold night is the better tradition. Bars run very late and the subway does not — last trains are around midnight, after which it's Kakao T.
