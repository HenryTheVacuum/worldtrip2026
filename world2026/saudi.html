01 The time audit

Five days,
two of them whole

Muscat to Riyadh is a short hop — about two hours, and you gain an hour crossing from UTC+4 into UTC+3 — so unlike most arrivals on this trip the 10th is genuinely half usable. The problem is the other end. Any version of this stop that includes AlUla spends two of the four remaining days getting to it and back, and a domestic flight eats a morning in each direction. What's left is the 12th, the 14th, and three afternoons.

Sunday is a working dayThe Saudi weekend is Friday–Saturday, so landing on Sunday 10 January puts you in a normal business day — shops open, traffic heavy, offices running. That's the good news. The bad news is that Sunday is the standard closing day for Riyadh's state museums: the National Museum and SAMoCA at JAX are both shut. Arrival day has to be Diriyah, Masmak or the Sky Bridge, none of which care what day it is.

No Friday in the window10–14 January runs Sunday to Thursday. You miss the long Jumu'ah midday closure entirely, which on a four-day stop is worth more than it sounds — Friday mornings in Riyadh are effectively dead until about 13:30.

Ten and a half hours of lightSunset is around 17:20 in Riyadh and 17:50 in AlUla. That sounds punishing until you notice that half the things worth doing here — At-Turaif, Elephant Rock, the Sky Bridge, Boulevard City — only open in the late afternoon anyway. Saudi front-loads its mornings with heat for most of the year and has built its evenings accordingly. See section 05.

02 Three things that don't move

The calendar
is against you

Two of these are about dates you can't shift without moving the whole westbound chain, and one is a piece of paperwork that has to be solved in Toronto. None of them is fatal. All three change what you should book.

Winter at Tantora ends 9 January

Missed by two days

AlUla's flagship festival — concerts at Maraya, Shorfat Tantora performances from the balconies of Old Town, the AlManshiyah carnival, pop-up dining across the oasis — runs 17 December 2026 to 9 January 2027. You land in Riyadh on the 10th and reach AlUla on the 11th. The tents come down as you arrive.

Then it goes the other way. Ultra Gobi AlUla, a 400 km desert ultramarathon, runs 13–20 January — the first international edition, and it starts the day you leave. Runners and crew will already be in town on the 12th and 13th, competing for the same limited bed stock.

What to doNothing, unless the whole Gulf leg shifts three days earlier — and it can't, because the UAE stop is anchored to New Year's. So reset expectations rather than the dates: mid-January AlUla is the archaeology, not the festival. Hegra, Dadan and the oasis are all open year-round and all better without a concert crowd. Book beds early anyway — Ultra Gobi is pulling on the same nights.

Riyadh–AlUla is not a daily flight

And the sources disagree

Every booking site calls RUH–ULH "daily." The detailed schedules do not agree with that or with each other. FlightsFrom's sample week shows five weekly departures from Riyadh; Wego's schedule, updated 2 September 2026, shows five on a different pattern. Both agree there is no Wednesday departure out of Riyadh, and they disagree about Sunday.

What they do agree on: Monday (flynas XY159, 10:20), Tuesday (Saudia SV1574, 09:15) and Thursday (SV1576, 10:15) all run. Coming back, flynas XY160 leaves AlUla at 12:50 every day but Saturday, and on a Wednesday it is the only flight of the day.

What to doBook the specific flight before you book the hotel, and treat the return as the fragile leg. Do not fly AlUla → Riyadh → Cairo on the same day. One 12:50 departure with no backup, feeding a four-hour international flight, is how a country gets deleted. Come back on the 13th and keep the 14th as buffer.

Canada's advisory vs. your insurance

Already flagged, still open

Global Affairs Canada has Saudi Arabia at "avoid non-essential travel", updated 9 September 2026 — the same level and the same wording as the UAE and Oman. Most Canadian travel-medical policies exclude claims arising in a country already under a Level 3 advisory at the time of purchase.

The eVisa's bundled medical cover does not fill the gap: it is emergency treatment only, capped around SAR 100,000, with no repatriation, no cancellation and no baggage.

What to doThis is stops 19 through 22, not just this one, and it's covered in full in the project note flag-gulf-advisory-insurance. Short version: buy a policy that names Level 3 destinations as covered, get it in writing, and check the war/terrorism carve-out doesn't reopen the same hole.

03 The split

Two nights
in AlUla, or three

Riyadh and AlUla are 880 km apart and the only sane link is the plane. Four nights have to divide between them, and the flight days do most of the deciding for you. Both shapes below assume the same Monday outbound — it's the return that separates them, and the return is where the risk lives.

Recommended

Two nights, buffered

Riyadh ×1→ AlUla ×2→ Riyadh ×1→ CAI

Out on flynas Monday morning, back on the Wednesday 12:50, and the whole of Thursday sitting in Riyadh as slack before the Cairo flight. AlUla gets one uninterrupted day plus two half-days at either end, which is enough for Hegra, Dadan and Jabal Ikmah, the Old Town and the oasis, Elephant Rock after dark and a Maraya tour — the core of the place.

What Thursday buys you: either the National Museum and Murabba Palace — the museum opens until 22:00 on Thursdays specifically — or an Edge of the World sunset run. It also means a cancelled AlUla flight costs you a day, not the country.

Why not skip Riyadh and give AlUla the whole stop

Because you can't get to AlUla without Riyadh — there is no international service to ULH from anywhere on your route, and no direct ULH–Cairo flight. Riyadh is the door, so you're paying for at least one night there whatever you decide. Given that, the door is worth opening: Diriyah's At-Turaif is a genuine UNESCO site and one of the best evenings in the Gulf, the metro will take you from the airport to the middle of the city for SAR 4, and the National Museum is free.

The reverse trade — dropping AlUla and giving Riyadh four days — is only worth considering if the domestic flights won't line up. Riyadh is an interesting city but it is not a four-day city, and Hegra is the reason this stop is on the itinerary at all.

What's cut either way

Jeddah and the Red Sea. The old town at Al-Balad is the most atmospheric urban fabric in the country and it's a completely different Saudi from Riyadh. It also needs its own two nights and a third flight. Not in five days.

Qiddiya. Six Flags opened 31 December 2025, about an hour outside Riyadh, from SAR 325. It's a good theme park. It is not what you flew here for.

Sharaan Nature Reserve and Gharameel stargazing. Both are AlUla's best desert experiences and both are three-night propositions. Worse: as of September 2026 the official Sharaan tours are marked "coming back soon" and the Gharameel listing shows a gap over the winter — check before you build a night around either.

04 Day by day

Both versions,
laid out

The shape of a Saudi day is not the shape of a European one. Mornings are for the archaeology and the museums, the middle of the day is dead, and everything with atmosphere — the mud-brick districts, the rock formations, the terraces — switches on around 16:00 and runs to midnight. Plan backwards from dusk.

Everything
opens late

This is the single most useful thing to know before you build a Saudi itinerary, and no guidebook leads with it. The AlUla night is the surpriseBoth places sit around 21°C in the afternoon, which is perfect. But AlUla drops to about 4°C overnight and has recorded below zero in January. Elephant Rock after dark, a desert camp, or a stargazing run at Gharameel all need a real insulated jacket, a hat and gloves — not the fleece that got you through Bangkok. This is the coldest you will have been since Nepal.

Prayer times still pause thingsThe 2021 ruling let businesses stay open through prayers, and malls, hotels, pharmacies and airports do. Plenty of smaller shops and restaurants still shut for 20–30 minutes at each of the five. In January, Maghrib falls around 17:30 in Riyadh — right when you'd want dinner. Install a prayer-times app and don't build a tight restaurant-to-anything sequence across it.

Rain is not a factorRiyadh averages about 5 mm across the whole of January; AlUla has roughly a 2% chance of a wet day. Nothing on this page needs a weather contingency, which after Sri Lanka and the Maldives is a novelty.

Where the numbers are shaky

The eVisa fee. No official source publishes the breakdown. Third-party totals range from SAR 402 to SAR 535, most commonly quoted as SAR 300 government fee plus SAR 235 mandatory medical insurance. The checkout total at visa.visitsaudi.com is the only figure that counts — budget CAD 200 and expect to be pleasantly surprised.

At-Turaif entry. Long-standing price is SAR 50, fully redeemable against food or drink at almost any Bujairi Terrace outlet. Multiple 2026 visitors report SAR 100, also redeemable. Budget SAR 100 and treat it as a dinner credit rather than a ticket.

The Kingdom Centre Sky Bridge. SAR 69 in one 2026 source, SAR 138 including VAT in another. Budget the higher one.

The AlUla hop-on-hop-off shuttle. Widely cited at SAR 150 by secondary guides, but the official Experience AlUla page marks the shuttle "coming back soon" — i.e. suspended. Don't plan transport around it.

Getting around, and the AlUla transport hole

Riyadh is solved. All six metro lines are open, Line 4 runs into all three airport terminal stations, and a two-hour ticket covering metro and bus is SAR 4 — a three-day pass is SAR 20. Apple Pay and contactless work at the gates. Uber and Careem both operate and are cheap: airport to downtown runs about SAR 85–110. The one gap is Diriyah, which the metro does not reach and won't until the early 2030s — that's a SAR 20–30 taxi from the nearest station.

AlUla is not solved. Taxis exist only at the airport and are first-come-first-served. Uber and Careem have almost no cars. The official Darb AlUla ride-hail app exists but publishes no fares anywhere. The public shuttle is suspended. What actually works is that the official tours include transport from Winter Park — so book the tours and let them be your transport, stay somewhere central, and budget for a car or a driver to cover the gaps. A private full-day sedan starts around SAR 999; independent travellers report finding drivers nearer SAR 600.

ConnectivitySTC, Mobily and Zain all have strong 5G in Riyadh and decent coverage in AlUla. A local SIM needs your passport at the counter. WhatsApp and FaceTime messaging always works; voice and video calling is still intermittently blocked on local networks despite being officially unblocked since 2017. A travel eSIM routing through an international carrier often gets calls through where a local SIM won't — worth the few dollars.

Mecca and MedinaBoth are closed to non-Muslims inside the haram boundaries, marked by checkpoints on the highways. This has no effect on you: the restriction is on ground entry to those cities, not on airspace, and Riyadh–AlUla flies well north of both. If you were driving it — you aren't — the route via Hail keeps you clear anyway.

Getting in from MuscatRoughly two hours, and you gain an hour going from UTC+4 to UTC+3, so a mid-morning departure puts you in Riyadh before lunch. Take the metro in: Line 4 serves all three terminal stations and SAR 4 buys you two hours of unlimited travel. It's the best-value thing in the country.

Getting out to CairoNot a worry. Nine carriers fly RUH–CAI — EgyptAir, Saudia, flynas, flyadeal, Nile Air, Air Cairo and others — roughly 177 flights a week, about four hours. If something slips, there is another plane. This is the opposite of the AlUla problem, and it's why the buffer day belongs in Riyadh.
