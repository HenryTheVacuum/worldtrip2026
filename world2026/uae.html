01 The time audit

Five days
is three days

Mumbai to Dubai is about 3h25 in the air and you gain ninety minutes crossing from UTC+5:30 to UTC+4 — one of the few legs on this trip where the clock works for you. Twelve nonstops a day on five carriers means you can land whenever you like. Even so: arrival day is half a day, departure day is a morning, and what's left is three full days to cover two cities that are ninety minutes apart.

No jet lag to spendUnlike Tokyo, this one gives you nothing. An hour and a half is a late lunch, not a reset. You arrive on Mumbai time, which is close enough to Gulf time that you will sleep normally and wake normally — so the dawn trick that carried the Asian legs does not apply here. Build the days around heat and light instead: outdoors early and after dark, indoors and air-conditioned from about eleven to four.

January is the reason this stop worksHighs around 24°C, lows near 14°C, a 6% chance of rain on any given day, and humidity that is only oppressive about 2% of the month. Sunrise about 07:05, sunset about 17:45 — short days, so the outdoor hours are genuinely limited. This is the one season the Gulf is walkable, which is exactly why it is also the most expensive.

DXB is the good airportYou land and leave from Dubai International, not Al Maktoum out in the desert. Metro Red Line runs to both DXB terminals 1 and 3 and puts you in Downtown or Deira in about 20–30 minutes for a few dirhams. If a schedule change tries to move the Muscat leg to DWC, push back — it is an hour further from everything.

02 Two things that don't move

Read these
before booking

One of these breaks a line in the trip plan outright. The other is a brand-new piece of infrastructure that will probably be excellent and cannot yet be booked with confidence.

Flag 01 — You miss New Year's Eve by three days

The plan says something impossible

The trip note for this stop reads "see: New Year's fireworks off the Burj." You land on 3 January. The Burj Khalifa show is a single night — 31 December — and on that night you are in Mumbai, where the plan deliberately put you for New Year. Both things cannot be true.

This is not a scheduling near-miss that a different flight fixes. It is a three-day gap and the only lever is the India/UAE boundary itself: India currently runs 29 Dec–3 Jan with Ajanta and Ellora in it, which is a long overland haul from Mumbai that needs its days.

What to doRecommended: let it go and correct the note. New Year in Mumbai — Marine Drive, the Gateway, the city's own noise — is the better version of that night anyway, and Dubai on 31 December means peak-of-peak room rates, closed roads around Downtown, and crowd control that starts in the afternoon. What you still get on 3–7 January: the Dubai Shopping Festival, which runs 13 Dec 2026 – 19 Jan 2027 and is squarely inside your window; the Dubai Fountain every 30 minutes from 18:00; and post-New-Year hotel rates, which fall hard from about 2 January.

If the fireworks genuinely matter, the swap is to move Dubai earlier and India later — but that costs you New Year in Mumbai and squeezes the Ajanta/Ellora overland days, which are the tightest part of the India stop. It is a bad trade for fifteen minutes of pyrotechnics you can watch on a screen.

Flag 02 — Etihad Rail is three months old and both stations are suburban

Plan the bus, hope for the train

The UAE now has intercity passenger rail. Service opened 30 June 2026 between Abu Dhabi and Fujairah; the Dubai Al Yalayis station opened 30 September 2026, connected to Jumeirah Golf Estates metro station on the Red Line by a 400-metre footbridge. Dubai to Abu Dhabi is quoted at 57 minutes.

Two catches. First, as of September 2026 fares and frequency for the Dubai–Abu Dhabi leg had not been published — only the founding Abu Dhabi–Fujairah fares, from AED 55 Comfort and AED 120 Premium. Second, both ends are on the outskirts: Al Yalayis is out by Dubai South, and the Abu Dhabi end is Mohamed bin Zayed City, not the Corniche. That 57 minutes is station to station, with metro and taxi legs at both ends.

The fallback that already worksBus E101, Ibn Battuta metro station to Abu Dhabi Central, AED 25, about two hours, every 30 minutes. E100 runs the same fare from Al Ghubaiba in old Dubai. Nol card only — these buses do not take cash. Buy and load a Nol the hour you arrive.

Check the Etihad Rail site when you book accommodation. If Dubai–Abu Dhabi fares are published and the timetable suits, take the train; if not, the bus is proven and the whole plan below works either way.

03 The open question

Abu Dhabi:
night or day trip

Four nights, two cities, one airport. Abu Dhabi used to be a comfortable day trip — mosque, palace, Louvre, home. That stopped being true in December 2026, when the Guggenheim opened on Saadiyat Island and gave the island four major museums inside a ten-minute walk. Both shapes below fit 3–7 January.

Recommended

One night in Abu Dhabi

Dubai ×2→ Abu Dhabi ×1→ Dubai ×1→ DXB

You get the two things a day trip cannot buy: the Grand Mosque after dark, when the white marble goes blue-grey under the floodlights and the coach parties have gone, and a whole unhurried morning on Saadiyat for the Guggenheim and the Zayed National Museum.

Coming back to Dubai for the last night is not sentiment — it is insurance. Your onward flight leaves DXB, and you do not want a two-hour intercity transfer standing between you and the gate on departure morning.

If you hate moving hotels

Dubai base, one long day out

One room for four nights, bags never packed, and a 06:30–23:00 day trip on the Tuesday. It genuinely works: mosque at opening, Qasr Al Watan midday, Saadiyat all afternoon, last bus or train back.

Where to sleep in Dubai, given three full days

Deira or Bur Dubai if you want the city that existed before 1990 — the creek, the souks, the abras, cheap excellent food, and metro at the door. Rooms here are a fraction of Downtown prices and the tourism dirham is lower because the hotels are lower-rated. This is the pick.

Downtown / Business Bay if the Burj and the fountain are the point and you want to walk to them at night. Convenient, expensive, and surrounded by mall rather than city.

Dubai Marina / JBR is the beach option and the furthest from everything old. On a four-night stop it costs you metro time you do not have. Skip.

05 What's actually worth doing

Do, maybe,
don't bother

The UAE is unusually good at selling you experiences that are expensive, indoor, and identical to something you could do in any large city. The do list below is weighted hard towards the things that are only here: the creek, the desert, the mosque, and an island that has quietly become one of the densest museum districts on earth.

06 The luckiest timing on the trip

Saadiyat,
three weeks in

Abu Dhabi has been building a museum district on Saadiyat Island for twenty years, and almost all of it has landed in the last eighteen months. Guggenheim Abu Dhabi opens on 11 December 2026 — Frank Gehry's building, the largest in the Guggenheim network, 30 galleries and 11,600 m² of interior space, focused on art from the 1960s onward from West Asia, North Africa and South Asia. You arrive three weeks and two days later.

Fig. 2 — When Saadiyat's museums opened 2017 → 2027 · your window marked

OpenYour window · 3–7 Jan 2027

What this actually means for the day

Four serious institutions inside a short walk of each other, and three of them did not exist when most guidebooks on your shelf were written. Louvre Abu Dhabi (AED 70, under-18s free) is still the one to see if you only see one — Jean Nouvel's perforated dome throws a "rain of light" that is worth the ticket on its own, and the galleries stay open to 20:30 Friday to Sunday. Zayed National Museum opened in December 2025. teamLab Phenomena is the digital-art barn, and duplicates a lot of what you will already have seen at teamLab in Tokyo on stop 01 — which is exactly why it is a "maybe" and not a "do".

The three-week-old caveat. A museum that new will have queues, unfinished wayfinding, timed-entry teething and possibly a gallery or two still dark. Book the Guggenheim the day tickets open rather than the week you fly, and treat the published opening hours as provisional until you are standing there.

07 The bill

The room rate
is not the room rate

There is no income tax here and there is no departure tax, but hotels carry a stack of charges that are quoted separately and land at checkout. In Dubai the stack adds roughly 25–30% to a headline rate; in Abu Dhabi, roughly 18–20%. Over four nights in the first week of January — peak season — that difference is real money. The service charge is not a tipIt goes to the property, not the staff, and it is mandatory. Tipping on top is customary but genuinely optional — 10% in a restaurant that has not already added a service charge, a few dirhams for a porter.

VAT refunds are real and worth it5% VAT on retail purchases is refundable to tourists at the airport through the Planet system, on minimum spends, with the goods unused and the tag validated before you check the bag. If you buy anything substantial during the Shopping Festival, allow an extra 30 minutes at DXB.

Cash is barely neededCards work almost everywhere. The exceptions are the abra (AED 1, cash only), some old-souk stalls, and small change for the mosque abaya deposit. AED 200–300 covers the whole stop.

AlcoholLegal for non-Muslim tourists in licensed hotels, bars and restaurants, and Dubai dropped its 30% municipality tax on alcohol sales. It is expensive regardless. Drinking in public or being drunk in public remains a criminal offence — this is the rule people actually get caught by.

WeatherHighs around 24°C, lows near 14°C, sea about 22°C, and rain on roughly 6% of days. Mornings can be foggy in January — genuinely thick, occasionally enough to delay flights. Bring one warm layer for the desert at night, where it drops fast after sunset.

Getting around DubaiMetro Red and Green lines cover most of what you want and cost AED 3–7.50 a journey. Taxis are metered, cheap by Canadian standards, flag-fall about AED 12 and roughly AED 2.50/km. Careem and Uber both work. Old Dubai is genuinely walkable; new Dubai is not.

The desertBook an evening safari from a reserve operator rather than the cheapest dune-bashing package — from about AED 150 per person for the shared version. The difference between a convoy of forty 4x4s and a quiet camp on a conservation reserve is the difference between a theme park and the reason you came.

OnwardDXB to Muscat is 1h10, about 35 flights a week across Emirates, flydubai and Oman Air, departures spread from 02:15 to 22:45. This is the easiest connection on the entire westbound leg — verify the exact operated dates rather than trusting a schedule site's day labels.
